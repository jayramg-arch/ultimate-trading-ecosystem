Quality of zones:

1) Strength of departure (strength of the leg-out or leg-out+Follow-through, or leg-out with gap up/ down.
2) Number of candles in the base (you have already included this)
3) Alignment with higher timeframe zones
4) Arrival Style: How price returns matters as much as the zone itself. A fast, high-ATR drop into a demand zone often results in a sharp, immediate rejection. Conversely, a slow, stair-stepping compression into a zone suggests momentum is absorbing the level, increasing the risk of a bleed-through.
5) Volume & Order Flow Footprints: A pristine zone shows distinct volume expansion on the departure leg. Upon arrival, order flow should reveal delta divergence—for instance, aggressive selling volume drying up or being actively absorbed exactly as it hits the demand base.
6) Dynamic Support/Resistance Alignment: When a static price zone overlaps with a key dynamic level—such as the $EMA_{20}$ tracking cleanly on the daily timeframe—it creates a high-probability nexus.
7) Fibonacci Confluence: Zones that align with the 50% to 61.8% retracement levels of the macro leg provide institutional discount (for demand) or premium (for supply) pricing.
8) Proximity to EMA20: Price should be extended from the zone (for DZ, price is quite above the zone), but not away from the zone (price not below the DZ). Also, if EMA20 is neaby the zone, then the zone is stronger.
