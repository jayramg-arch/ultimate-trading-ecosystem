01Why it exists

Section Four sits permanently against the 100,256 compiled-token ceiling, so every diagnostic it ever carried was eventually cut to buy a decision field: the geometry classifier went for the pullback-aware gate, the Wyckoff tiers collapsed to one DIST tag, the setups S3 and S6 went whole. Those cuts are the contents of this file.

It was built from the S/R + Trendline Lab rather than from scratch — that file already carried the canonical pivots, the S/R clustering engine, the trendline fitters and the drawing block, all tested against hand-drawn lines. S5 is that lab plus the three things it lacked: a geometry read rebuilt on TradingView's own published criteria, a real footprint, and a panel in S4's grammar so the two read as one instrument.

02The second chart — not negotiable

Measured 6–8 September

A 55-line footprint feeder ran clean on its own chart while S4, holding two floats from it, threw “Memory limits exceeded” on the same chart — and removing the feeder cleared it with no recompile. request.footprint() retains per-bar volume-row objects, and Pine meters runtime memory per chart, so the two cannot share one. Loading S5 beside S4 kills S4's panel.

So the layout is two charts, symbol-synced: S4 with the dashboard, Unified, the Zigzag and the catalyst bridge on one; S5 alone on the other. The reviewer script reads both and merges them, refusing if the two tabs disagree on symbol or timeframe.

calc_bars_count is pinned at 1500 because bar count is the one lever that actually bounds what the footprint hoards — ticks per row was tried at 20 and 200 and changed nothing, since the cost is retention, not row size. If the memory error ever returns on S5's own chart: drop that first (800, then 400), and turn the footprint off second.

Two optional input.source inputs survive from the Lab — the S4 Trendline Tool bridge — and they are off unless you point them at that companion. Like every source, they unbind on recompile; a quiet Trendlines row after a compile is that, not a fault.

The twelve shapes

Ascending · Descending · Symmetrical triangle · Rising · Falling wedge · Rising · Falling channel · Bullish · Bearish flag · Bullish · Bearish pennant · Range / rectangle. Eight of TradingView's sixteen. The pole is what was missing — without it every falling channel after a two-ATR up-move read as bearish, and every pennant as directionless.

Status and target

AWAITING — price inside the boundaries → BROKEN UP · target close + base height or BROKEN DOWN · target close − base height. That is TradingView's own state machine and its height projection, printed so the number can be checked rather than believed.

The apex — v1.4, 16 September

Shape │ withdrawn - apex passed 7b ago - the coil has resolved, no live boundaries

Boundaries │ upper 4,961.20 (0.4x ATR) · lower 4,930.05 (0.2x ATR) · width 0.4x ATR · 82% to apex (late - TV wants the break before ~75%) · spans …

The inverted pair that got Geometry withheld on 11 September — TITAN, 125-minute, upper 4928 below lower 4968 — was not a fitting error. Two converging lines cross at the apex, and projected past it they swap sides; the panel was printing that crossed pair as live boundaries. v1.4 solves the apex bar from the two line equations and treats it the way TradingView's pattern pages do: a triangle, wedge or pennant ends at its apex, so a shape whose lines have met has resolved one way or the other whether or not a break was seen. Past the apex — or with the boundaries inverted for any other reason — the shape is withdrawn and the Shape row says why, instead of falling back to a bare “—”.

While the shape is live the Boundaries row now carries % to apex — how far through the pattern price is, from the earliest anchor to the crossing. Past 75% it is tagged late: TradingView's own guidance is that a valid breakout comes before roughly three quarters of the way to the apex; later than that the coil has lost its energy and a break is more often a drift out than a move. Parallel channels and rectangles have no apex and print nothing here.

Two design errors, both caught because the panel named its own failure

Fit-then-reject does not work. The Lab's best-line fitter returns the line threading the most pivots, which leaves pivots on both sides of it, so an interior close breaches it almost always — bolting TV's validation onto that silenced the classifier on a full pool. TV anchors to two pivots and validates the interior. The pair search now runs newest-first and takes the first that survives.

The two boundaries must cover the same window. Found independently, an upper anchored 25–12 bars ago and a lower 39–33 bars ago share nothing, and “width at the start versus width at the end” between segments that never coexisted is arithmetic on unrelated things — which is how a wedge measured as broadening 55%. Overlap is now gated and the Boundaries row prints it.

⚖ MARGINAL, and the one adaptation that is not TradingView's

A knife-edge marker fires when a slope sits within 20% of the flat threshold — one name read a base slope of 0.47× against a 0.50× threshold and asserted the result with full confidence. And the interior-close test came from a third-party account, not TV's pages; it is labelled as this file's adaptation in the source and is the first place S5 would diverge.

04bI · Chart patterns — drawn, confirmed, never graded (v1.8; reviewer + read v1.9, 27 Oct)

Chart pattern │ Inverse head & shoulders · CONFIRMED 3b ago at 1,234.50 · target 1,301.00 · no retest yet · +2 older on the chart

Eight patterns, drawn on the chart with their swing legs, a dashed neckline, a dotted measured-move target and a label: DB / DT double bottom and top, TB / TT triple, iH&S / H&S head and shoulders, C&H cup with handle and VCP·nT (n contractions). They come from a zigzag of the same 5/5 pivots the geometry classifier reads. Each pattern is a fixed sequence of the newest swings: a double bottom is low, high, low, and an inverse head and shoulders is shoulder, armpit, head, armpit, shoulder.

Visual aid only. Nothing here gates or scores, and nothing reaches S4's GO or alert. Since v1.9 the pattern is explained in prose in S5's own read and is shown to the AI reviewer.

Reading a pattern on the chart

  • Legs: the zigzag through the pattern's swings.
  • Neckline (dashed): the confirmation line. A close beyond it confirms the pattern.
  • Target (dotted): the pattern's height projected from the breakout. It is a reference for room, not a plan target. S4's canon T1/T2 still set the plan.
  • Label and tooltip: code and state on the label. Hover for the swings, height, primary trend and the confirmation, invalidation and target levels.

Timing and housekeeping

  • A pattern appears about five bars after its last swing, because a 5/5 pivot is only known five bars later.
  • When the newest swings fit more than one pattern, the larger one wins: inverse H&S, then triple bottom, then VCP, then double bottom (tops mirror this; a cup is checked before a double top).
  • A triple bottom replaces the double bottom it grew out of. A swing that extends further removes any pattern still forming on it.
  • Closed bars only, so nothing is drawn on the forming bar. Patterns older than 300 bars drop off, and at most six stay on the chart.
MarkMeaning
?Forming. The swings are in place but no close has broken the confirmation line yet. It is a possibility, not a signal
✓Confirmed by a close through the confirmation line (Bulkowski's rule for each of them)
✓↩Confirmed, then pulled back to the breakout level and held. This is the house entry: the retest, not the breakout
✓🎯Reached the measured target
✗ · ⌛Failed: closed beyond the pattern's extreme before breaking out. Expired: no breakout within one pattern-width (at least 10 bars)
✗ bustedConfirmed, then closed back beyond the extreme within one pattern-life of the breakout
↺Counter-trend: bullish under a falling 30-WMA, or bearish over a rising one. Dimmed by default; can be hidden

What a pattern must clear. Shape alone has little edge (the same finding as the candlestick work behind Add-ons M3), so each pattern also needs:

  • A move into it. A bottom must follow a decline, so the swing before it sits above the neckline. A top must follow an advance.
  • Size in ATR. At least 2× ATR from neckline to extreme, so it means the same on 75m and on Daily.
  • Proportion.
    • Double and triple bottoms and tops: equal within 0.75× ATR.
    • Head and shoulders: shoulders level within 40% of the height, the head beyond both by 0.5× ATR, and the wider side at most 2.5× the narrower.
    • Cup: 12–50% deep, at least 30 bars wide, with the low in the middle 60% and at least 20% of its bars in the bottom third (a U, not a V). The handle must stay in the upper half.
    • VCP: each pullback at most 0.8× the one before, highs not rising, the last pullback 10% or less, and only above a rising 30-WMA.
  • Confirmation. A close beyond the confirmation line. For head and shoulders this follows Bulkowski's rule for a sloped neckline: close through the neckline, or through the right armpit when the neckline slopes the wrong way.

The tooltip shows the swings, the height, the primary trend (S4's 30-WMA 2x2), and the levels for confirmation, invalidation and target. It also adds pattern notes:

  • VCP: the depth of each contraction and whether volume dried up.
  • Cup: its roundness.
  • Inverse head and shoulders: Bulkowski's figures (65% throw back to the neckline; 71% reach the target).
How often they appear

A Python mirror on 25 Daily charts (29,934 bars) found about 21 patterns per 1,000 bars across all eight types. That is about six on a 300-bar Daily chart, which is also the default cap. Of 40 confirmed inverse head-and-shoulders patterns, 30 reached the full measured target. This is a sanity check on the detector, not evidence of an edge. Nothing here has been tested as a filter.

Open item #11 — the rectangle rule

The classifier now applies TradingView's own rectangle rule first: every pivot within Permissible Deviation (15%) of a horizontal edge, measured against the height of the range, on 4, 3 or 2 pivots per side. The old test compared each slope with a fixed 0.5× ATR, so on a tall range ordinary noise between two highs read as a downslope and the range came out a symmetrical triangle. A rectangle's boundaries are also held flat now, so no apex is projected for it.

04cI · How long a chart pattern counts

A chart pattern is structure, so it stays live longer than a candlestick: roughly as long as it took to build. These are the windows in the code.

WindowBarsWhat happens
Detection lag5A swing is only known 5 bars after it forms (5/5 pivots), so a new pattern is already 5 bars old when it first appears
Forming → expired ⌛1× the pattern's width, at least 10No breakout within that many bars of the last swing (input Unconfirmed: expire after). A cup with handle gets about half the cup's width, at least 10, because only the handle is left to form
Retest ↩20 after the breakoutA bar back within 0.5× ATR of the breakout level that closes on the right side of it (inputs Retest: within / back within). That is the retest entry
Bustedone pattern-life after the breakoutA close back beyond the void level inside that span. After it the pattern no longer applies
Drawn on the chart300Older patterns drop off; at most six stay

What each state means for a trade

StateRead
? formingA base, not a trade, until a close through the confirmation line
✓ confirmed, 0–20 bars, no retestThe pullback to the breakout level is the entry. That matches the house retest convention (23-Jul A/B), not chasing the breakout bar
✓↩ retested and heldThe cleanest state: the breakout level has been tested and defended
✓🎯 target reachedThe measured move is done. Don't enter on the pattern
✗ busted · ⌛ expired · ✗ failedIgnore it for entries, or read it as a warning

A candlestick (Commander Add-ons M3, Doc 43 §10b) is a one-to-three-bar timing event and stops counting after 5 bars. When the two disagree, the chart pattern carries more weight.

Bars, not days

Every age here is counted in bars of the chart it is drawn on, so read it on the trade's own clock. A positional trade is decided on the Daily close: 3 bars is about 3 sessions, 5 bars about a week. A swing is timed on 75m or 125m: 3 bars is half a day to a day, and an intraday mark older than about a day says nothing about the setup.

Not fitted to this desk's data. The 1× width expiry and the 20-bar retest window are standard textbook practice (Bulkowski's throwback and pullback figures); the patterns are a visual aid that never gates, so these are reading rules, not tested edges.

05II · Levels — rebuilt on the reference methods (v1.7, 22 Oct)

Resistance │ 966.00 ·M fresh (1.4x ATR) Support │ 913.00 ·D fresh (0.8x ATR)

Trendlines │ support 890.87 (1.8x ATR) · resistance 978.30 (1.9x ATR)

Why it changed. The old engine kept the two nearest pivot clusters per side and deleted any level price had closed through twice. The levels a trader draws are the opposite: shelves price has traded back and forth across. On BAJFINANCE 125m the 975–985 shelf, closed through and retested for weeks, was never drawn, while a lone pivot at 900 was. The auto trendline had the same kind of fault. It was an envelope that had to sit under every pivot in a 16-pivot pool, so it drew a falling "support" through a decline's lows.

S/R now follows LonesomeTheBlue's Support Resistance Channels (open source, MPL-2.0, the most-used S/R script on TradingView), adapted to the house rules:

  1. Every pivot high and low in the last 290 bars seeds a band. Other pivots within 5% of the 300-bar range join it, highs and lows together, so a level that flipped role keeps its history.
  2. Strength = 20 per pivot + 1 per bar whose high or low entered the band. Bar touches are what lift a congestion shelf above a lone pivot.
  3. The strongest bands that do not overlap win. From those, the strongest N each side of price, plus the band price is inside, if any. (LonesomeTheBlue keeps the strongest overall; on BAJFINANCE 125m all six sat above price and no support was drawn.)
  4. The line sits on the band edge facing price: the top of a support, the bottom of a resistance. That is the first price the market meets, which is your wick-extreme rule. The band itself is shaded. Where two timeframes' bands overlap, only the higher timeframe is drawn.

It runs on Monthly, Weekly and Daily, and on the chart's own timeframe when intraday (new: a 75m chart now sees its own shelves). Each timeframe bands its own pivots at the canonical lengths (M 1 · W 5 · D 2 · intraday 2). The heavy part runs only on the last few closed bars, never per tick.

Tests still weaken a level. Strength ranks which levels are drawn. Tests grade them: the pivots on the level's current side since price last closed through it. A flip resets the count. Six tests is MTTWR, primed to break. A label reads, for example, D · Resistance 986.50 · tested×3 · s320; R→S / S→R appears only when the flip happened in the last 20 bars.

Trendlines now use pivot pairs, as LonesomeTheBlue's Trend Lines and the Adam Moradi tutorial do. Support joins a higher low to an earlier pivot low; resistance joins a lower high to an earlier pivot high. A line is valid only while no close has crossed it since its first anchor (wicks may pierce). Among valid lines, the one through the most pivots wins, then the longest. Auto is on; the manual anchors and the S4 Trendline Tool bridge still work with auto off.

Prototype checked before the build on TradingView's own bars (BAJFINANCE Daily) and Dhan bars (BAJFINANCE 125m, TITAN / CUMMINSIND Daily, SAILIFE 75m). Not yet compared against your hand-drawn lines on a fresh chart. Send the ones it still gets wrong.

06III · Participation — the real delta

Order flow │ +56.773K buyers · cum 20b +1.455M · 383.212K vs 326.439K

Divergence · VA │ BULLISH - new 10b low on STRONGER flow · POC 895.00 VAH 900.00 VAL 890.00 · imb 1B/0S

The VA triplet is this bar's footprint — the value area inside the single read bar, not a multi-bar profile. Since 29 Sep the row says so, and S4's Volume Profile row is the profile of record (100 bars, 40 rows, 70%) for the reviewer's level check.

This is what S4's Arrival · Δ row approximates and this file measures. request.footprint() gives the per-row buy and sell volume of each bar, so the delta here is aggressor volume, not an intrabar close-position proxy. Verified on two symbols and two timeframes before it shipped.

FieldRead it as
Bar deltaBuyers minus sellers on this bar. Positive on a down bar at a level is absorption — buyers taking what sellers offer, the location confirmed. Negative on an up bar at a high is bleeding — the breakout is being sold into
cum 20bCumulative delta over the window. The trend of participation; a price grinding lower on a rising cumulative delta is being accumulated
buy vs sellThe raw split behind the delta, so a small delta on huge two-way volume reads differently from the same delta on nothing
DivergenceFires only at an extreme — a new 10-bar high or low — and asks whether flow agreed. A new low on stronger flow is bullish; a new high on weaker flow is the distribution tell. Otherwise it says so, and says flow and price are pointing opposite ways when they are
POC · VAH · VALThe bar's own auction levels from the footprint rows — the fairest price and the 70% value area. Inside it is rotation; a close above VAH on volume is acceptance
imb nB/nSRows where one side out-traded the other by the imbalance multiple (300%). Stacked buy imbalances at a low are a floor being defended
How the reviewer uses it

Delta breaks ties the price gates leave open — a borderline RV, a tested zone — and is read as one story with futures OI, the AVWAPs and the value area: short build-up plus absorption at VAL plus the low-anchor holding is trapped shorts on a defended floor. It grades. It does not veto on its own.

Order flow at the level — new in v1.6 (12 Oct)

Two rows added under Divergence · VA. They answer Jay's question: what do absorption, trapped traders, aggressive traders and big orders mean at an S/R level. S5 summarises the footprint rows of the last 3 closed bars and places each signal against its own S/R ladder (the nearest two levels each side on Daily, Weekly and Monthly, and since v1.7 on the chart timeframe too; S4's demand and supply zones are not visible on this chart). A level counts when the bar's low or high came within 0.5 × ATR of it, or pierced it and closed back.

At the level prints the facts with prices (the bar, its H/L/C, the zone share and delta, stacked rows, the later close that trapped someone, the big row). ORDER-FLOW READ prints the verdict, the level and its timeframe, how many bars ago, what it means and what voids it. If the footprint rows are too coarse for stacking to exist (fewer than 6 rows on the last bar), the row says so and suggests a Ticks per volume row value — row height is fixed per chart, because Pine needs a compile-time value.

Fitting the rows (12 Oct). The reviewer sets its own S5 tab's row height for every stock before it reads (ATR ÷ 10 ÷ the stock's tick size, from Dhan's scrip master — NSE ticks run from ₹0.01 to ₹5 by price), so a review always gets a usable read. On your S5 tab, click Set S5 Rows (Desktop / Start → Weinstein Commander, SET_S5_ROWS.bat) after changing symbol. At the default 200 ticks a TVSMOTOR 125m bar spanned 3 rows; fitted, it spans about 10.

Grades, never gates — with one exception

None of this can be backtested (TradingView-only, bar by bar), so it never moves GO, size or a stop, and holdings are untouched. The one exception lives in the reviewer: FLOW-GATE turns a model TAKE on a breakout entry into WAIT after a FAILED BREAKOUT at that entry level, within the last 2 bars, on a name you don't hold. It is pre-registered (docs/PREREG_flow_gate.md) and comes out if the names it held back do no worse than the TAKEs it left alone.

Three separate pools on purpose — the geometry read, the zones and the trendlines want different pivot strengths and had been sharing one.

PATTERN READ (v1.9) is the second line of the read: the newest chart pattern in prose, in two lines, what it is and its state, then what that means for a long. Forming: a base, not yet a breakout, with its void level. Confirmed: supports a long while price holds above the break. Target reached: the move is done and no longer argues for an entry. Failed, expired or busted: a warning. A bearish top argues against a new long and is a reason to tighten a holding. It is not geometry-classifier output, so the reviewer is shown it.
