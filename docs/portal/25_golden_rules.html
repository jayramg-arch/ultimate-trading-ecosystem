Part 1

The Ten Rules

If you read nothing else before the session, read these.

  1. The watchlists qualify. The board times. S4 is the plan of record.Never re-litigate qualification on the chart, and never take the plan from the board.
  2. Never buy the touch. Buy the confirmation.Alert at the level → wait for a CLOSED bar → act on that bar. Never a resting order at the level.
  3. A stop closer than 1×ATR(D) is not a stop.Any R-multiple measured against it is fiction. Re-price before you believe it.
  4. Invalidation is not the stop.Know both prices before you enter, and know which one you will act on.
  5. A GO is not a trade.The setup can be sound while the plan is wrong. Fix the entry, the stop or the target — or pass.
  6. Room decides tradeability.If T1 is the resistance you distrust, you don't have a trade — you have a breakout candidate.
  7. Sector and weekly trend are gates, not footnotes.A Stage 3/4 sector or a weekly downtrend changes what the same chart is worth.
  8. Read results per family × direction. Never pooled.Pooling has produced three false conclusions in this system already.
  9. Log the decision — especially the passes.A log that records only entries cannot tell you whether your instinct beats the system.
  10. Confidence comes from executed trades, not built features.When the urge is to build, check whether there is a trade you are avoiding.

Part 2

Before you look at a single chart

Do

  • Restart properly. STOP_COMMANDER.bat, then relaunch. python -m streamlit forks a child that keeps port 8501, so Ctrl+C leaves the OLD code serving.
  • Check the data is today's. The board prints As_Of and a provenance strip. A stale cache once froze 15 names on 9-day-old bars.
  • Rebuild after any Trigger-TF or X-Ray change. Cached rows predate it.
  • Confirm the source lists aren't empty. The header names header-only CSVs — that is how Hunter names silently vanished.

Don't

  • Don't assume a browser reload reloaded the code. It does not — only a process restart does.
  • Don't read a mid-session snapshot after the close, or a >15-minute-old one during the session. The AGE guard warns you.
  • Don't compare today's Overall against last week's screenshot. Stage, RS and RRG were all recalibrated on 18–19 Aug.

Part 4

Context — the gates that come before the chart

Do

  • Check the trend stack — weekly, daily, entry-TF. The best long is a dip inside a weekly uptrend. Down at all three is a counter-trend bounce: smaller size, first obstacle as target.
  • Respect the sector. Stage 4 = long inside a group being distributed, the most common way a good chart fails. Stage 3 = no tailwind.
  • Prefer leadership. Beating index and sector with rotation intact is the strongest single argument a name has.
  • Note the stage age. Weeks old is early but unproven; 30 weeks in, demand more from the entry.

Don't

  • Don't hold Stage 3 or Stage 4. The rule you are historically slowest on — RELIANCE sat as a known Stage-4 violation for weeks.
  • Don't gate on the RRG quadrant. Measured: the tradeable whitelist is worth +0.12pp at 4 weeks, +0.00pp at 12.
  • Don't call it leadership when rotation has rolled over. Strength being given back is not strength.
The two that survived

Only LEADING → LEADING and WEAKENING → LEADING were positive at both horizons in both windows. Those two are worth your eye on the chip. The rest is noise.

Part 5

Location and geometry

Where the money is actually lost.

Location

Do

  • Buy at a level, not a price. A zone gives an area and a distal that defines wrong. A line gives way without warning.
  • Know which kind. A pattern zone marks an imbalance — breaking it means the demand that caused the advance is gone. A pivot zone is a shelf — breaking it means a lower low. The pivot is weaker and scores nothing in confluence.
  • Tests WEAKEN a level. 1 = fresh · 2–5 = tested · 6+ = spent, and a breakout candidate.

Don't

  • Don't buy inside supply and call it a pullback. The only coherent long from inside supply is one paid for breaking it.
  • Don't buy extended. Beyond ~2.5×ATR from the daily EMA20 with no zone underneath, every stop is arbitrary and every dip looks like an entry.

Geometry — the most expensive recurring error

The evidence

CHOLAFIN, 18 Aug. Engine printed T1 5.6R. The stop was 13 points against an ATR(D) of 46.3 — 0.28×ATR. Re-priced against a 1×ATR stop, T1 was ~1.6R.

COFORGE, 19 Aug. 3.1R off a 0.33×ATR stop. Same fiction, next day.

Do

  • Measure every stop in ATR before believing any R. Under 0.5× the panel says so outright; under 1× treat the R as optimistic.
  • Set the stop from volatility, then let the reward fall where it falls. A 2R trade priced honestly beats a 5R trade on a stop that cannot survive a Tuesday.
  • Keep invalidation separate. Hard stop for the wick, closing-basis exit at the structural level for the thesis. The panel prints both.
  • Check room first. IN SUPPLY or under 1R to the first obstacle means the trade must pay before it reaches anything.

Don't

  • Don't accept the R the panel hands you. It derives the stop from the nearest structure without asking whether that structure is closer than a day's noise.
  • Don't set entry and invalidation a point apart. If being in and being wrong are the same price, wait for a deeper entry.
  • Don't widen a stop to flatter the R without re-sizing. Wider stop, smaller position, same rupee risk — or it is not the same trade.

Part 6

Trigger and entry

Your most expensive habit

Entering at the zone on the touch, skipping confirmation. This is the rule with the most evidence behind it and the one worth being rigid about.

Do

  • Alert at the level. Wait for a CLOSED bar. Then act.
  • Prefer the retest to the buy-stop. Your own A/B: buy-stop −0.02% mean matched alpha vs retest +0.38%, better in every family — and the retest filled 320 names vs 268.
  • Breaking a known ceiling? Wait for a closed bar above on volume, then buy-stop above that bar's high — or bid the retest of the level as new support.
  • Insist on volume — the V gate is not optional. The most reliable confirmation the panel offers, and what failed breakouts most often lack. What RV is: the current bar’s volume ÷ a rolling 50-bar average of the same series, computed on the timeframe of the chart you are looking at. On a 75m chart that is a 75m bar against the average 75m bar — so on an intraday chart RV is intraday volume. There is no separate non-intraday volume measure in S4 or the board. Read it against the clock: the same 1.0 means different things at 10:30 and at 13:00 (see §8c).

Don't

  • Never park a resting order at the level. Price tags it, fills you, closes back below — and you are long inside supply with the level overhead.
  • Don't treat a coil as a trigger. NR7 and inside bars are compression, not ignition.
  • Don't act on a dead-volume reclaim. VIJAYA's RV-0.2 fade-bounce was a skip.
  • Don't read "buy-limit at the trigger close" as a pullback when that close is the current bar — it fills at market, and the panel says so.
The one volume exception

At a demand zone in pullback context, thin volume is dry-up — sellers exhausted, which is what you want. It stops being a virtue the moment you need follow-through. The RV floor drops to 0.5 there for exactly this reason.

Part 7

Sizing, management and exits

Do

  • 1% risk per trade (0.25% new entries, 1% on pyramid adds), volatility-adjusted off the 14-day ATR.
  • Let the trail do the work. Over 203 POS trades: 88% exit on the trail, 11.8% at the initial stop, and only 8.4% ever reach 3R.
  • Use the R-canon — swing 2R/4R, positional 3R/5R, nothing under 2R. Half the position carries no target and rides.
  • Trail tighten-only, never loosen.

Don't

  • Don't re-tune the stop or trail on a hunch. Four studies have rejected tightening — the structural-SL variant had the best mean, passed the OOS gate, and was still rejected on median (−0.48R → −1.01R) and stop-outs (11.8% → 52.7%).
  • Don't hold a positional target on a swing-shaped trade. If structure says swing, book into strength.
  • Don't leave a position without a resting stop. 445 shares once sat uncovered.

Pre-trade gate 0 of 12 cleared

8b · When the alert fires on a name you have not armed

The one case the checklist above does not cover — it assumes you arrived at the chart deliberately. An alert can instead introduce you to a stock.

It is not a contradiction. Arming is a manual attention marker, not a gate. A qualified name that triggers before you got round to arming it is the alert doing the watching you didn't. The name is on the watchlist, so it is already qualified.

Spend 30 seconds on why first — the likeliest cause is a stale board. Is it rebuilt for this bar? Same timeframe as the alert? Then read the row:

Board rowDo
agrees, or behindTake it through the checklist — S4 is the plan of record.
INVALIDATEDStop. Board and S4 share the same stage 2×2, so they should not disagree — one has stale data. Reconcile before acting.
board unavailableS4 alone can evaluate the trade, but the cross-check is gone. Be stricter on geometry.
The real cost

Not the missing arm — the missing homework. An un-armed name is usually one you have not studied: its archetype, which scan qualified it, Bull or Recovery, and if Recovery whether the RFF fundamentals hold — which S4 cannot see. You would be doing that research at the trigger, under time pressure, which is where your worst entries came from.

The rule: take it if the checklist passes and you can answer "why is this name on the list at all" without guessing. If you cannot, the pass reason writes itself.

The structural fix is upstream. Arm names during the evening review, so a trigger arrives on a stock you have already thought about. The alert should tell you when — it should not be making the introduction.

8c · The alert marks a bar, not a state — and the first bar of the day lies

Three of the four gates are fixed at the trigger bar. Location is not.

GateNatureTrue an hour later?
P · patternstructural — a pattern formedyes

An alert can fire correctly at 10:30 and the panel read L· when you open it at 11:45. Neither is wrong. There is a second route too: a tested zone is deleted by the lifecycle, so the touch that fired the alert can consume the zone that justified it.

20 Aug — nine alerts on the 10:30 bar

By the time the panels were read, SONACOMS and COFORGE showed no location. SONACOMS still read P✓ L· V✓ B✓ with the AVWAP-BO at 840.90 pressed against price at 836.90 — it had drifted off the anchor it triggered on.

Arriving late, re-evaluate at the current bar. If location has gone, the entry premise has gone with it — the trade was buy at the level, and price is no longer at the level. It is also why the plan latches the trigger bar instead of re-deriving the entry from live price.

And treat the 10:30 cluster with suspicion — this one is MEASURED. S4 computes chart_rv = volume / sma(volume,50)[1], a rolling baseline that mixes every bar of the day. NSE intraday volume is U-shaped, so that single baseline is too low for the opening bar and too high for the midday ones. Over 55 board names × 90 days, 14,466 bar-observations:

75m bar closesmedian RVshare of session volumeV-gate pass ≥1.0

The V gate passes ~50% at the open and close, and ~18% midday — a 2.8× difference from time of day alone. So a 10:30 GO cleared the weakest volume test of the day, at the bar most exposed to auction noise; an 11:45 or 13:00 GO cleared one that only ~1 bar in 5 passes, which makes it stronger evidence on that dimension. 15:30 is as inflated as 10:30 and is post-close anyway. 125m is milder (36% / 17% / 37%) — wider bars average more of the session, an unanticipated second argument for running that set.

Scope

This measures the gate, not outcomes. It does not say 10:30 triggers lose money — that needs an intraday backtest, and none exists (the s4go replay is daily-bar). Read it as "the volume gate is not the same test at every hour", which is enough to change how much weight you give V at each bar. Latent fix, not yet made: an RV baseline per time-of-day slot. It is a signal change, so it gets measured before it ships.

8d · Pyramid ADDs — not all four, but location matters more

An add is a different trade. You already own it, it is already working, and the thesis is proven by the position. What you are buying is more of a winner at a good price — so the gates change weight.

GateNew entryADD
P · patternrequirednot required — the position is the thesis
V · volumerequirednot required — demanding RV ≥ 1 is wrong at a pullback, where dry-up is what you want
B · barrequiredkeep it — do not add on a collapsing bar
L · locationrequiredthe whole game — a bad add raises your average and turns a winner into a bigger loser

Three ADD-only checks that outrank all four: the stop must rise (the Chandelier raise applies to the whole position — if it cannot move up you are only adding risk) · combined heat and correlation, since an add concentrates exposure you already have · and is it actually working — ADD is for winners.

Correction — same day, 20 Aug

An earlier version of this rule said that on SONACOMS "pyramid said ADD while S4 said NOT AT LOCATION". That was wrong. pyramid_logic live said HOLD. The ADD +8.7% · 0.57R on the S4 panel comes from the v67 portfolio slot — a snapshot written by Sync-to-TV, not a live pyramid verdict. One panel field was read and a disagreement inferred between two engines that were not disagreeing.

The rule stands for a different reason. _at_location WAS a trend test wearing a location test's name — ltp > ema20 passes at any distance above the 20-EMA. It now carries an ATR extension cap (ADD_MAX_EXT_ATR, default 2.0) measured the way S4 measures it.

Measured on the live 17-position book, the cap changes nothing today — the two ADDs sit at 1.46× and 0.89× ATR above the 20-EMA. It guards a case that has not happened yet. 2.0 rather than 1.5 because 1.5 would have sat 0.04 ATR above a live ADD.

A rule that came out of getting it wrong: a value on the S4 panel is not always computed by S4. The v67 slot rows (MY TRADE / POSITION) are synced snapshots, as old as the last Sync-to-TV. When a portfolio field on the chart disagrees with the Pyramid page, the page is live and the chart is a photograph.

Part 9

Reading the system's own output

Do

  • Trust your chart read over the mechanical verdict. The engine is a measuring instrument; your eyes have a veto. It can talk you out of a trade — never into one.
  • Treat an unknown as unknown. ?, n/a, — mean data is absent, not that the answer is no. Every gate fails OPEN on missing data by design.
  • Believe the tags. ⚠role, ⧖D, ⚠unval, · RRG·, ⚖ all say the row is softer than it looks.

Don't

  • Don't read a number without asking which bar it came from. SYRMA read LEADING on confirmed weeks and WEAKENING with the forming week — one Monday session deciding the quadrant.
  • Don't take a confluence score as conviction. It grades; it does not gate.
  • Don't assume two panels showing the same field agree. When they disagree, find which bar and which producer — that is where the information is.

Part 10b

What the measurements actually say

Part 10 is how to read evidence. This is the evidence — every number from a run in validation_runs/, with its sample size attached. Where a result is thin, it says so.

Remember this one

Plan around +1% matched alpha per trade, and a MEDIAN trade that loses to the index. The selection edge is real and survives beta adjustment (median ex-ante beta 1.11). But it is a low-hit-rate, big-winner-carried profile — the beta-adjusted median is negative (POS-BO −2.49% IS, −3.44% OOS). Sizing and drawdown tolerance matter more than the mean implies. Any figure you remember above ~+1% comes from one exceptional stretch, May 2023 – Jan 2024.

Confirmation times entries badly — but selects them well

  • Entering on a buy-STOP above the confirmed GO bar erases the edge: +2.56% at the anchor close → −0.02% GO-timed, across every family, cell by cell. Not a composition artifact.
  • A retest limit is strictly better than a buy-stop in every family and fills 52 more names — now the default. But it recovers only ~0.4 of the 2.6pp gap; the residual is the confirmation-wait tax, and no entry tweak removes it.
  • Widening the stop does not rescue a GO-timed entry — it converts quick −2% shakeouts into slow, larger losses (win 34% → 30%, median −1.19% → −2.92%).
  • So the GO gate is a trade CLASSIFIER, not an entry optimizer. Use it to arm and focus; do not expect it to improve fills.

Gate behaviour worth knowing

  • The bar gate's errors are asymmetric, so it stays loose. It fires the alert: a false positive costs one glance, a false negative costs the trade because the name never surfaces. Tightening cut fills 314 → 241 and the removed trades were net winners. Proposed, measured and rejected three times.
  • RV is time-of-day biased. The volume gate passes 48% at 10:30, ~18% midday, 53% at 15:30 — a 2.8× swing from the clock alone, because RV divides by a baseline mixing every bar of the day. A burst of alerts at the close is partly that artifact.
  • Wyckoff failed twice — as a GO veto it was backwards (vetoed cohort +5.60% vs kept +0.52%), and as a score input it was null (held-out ρ +0.013, p 0.74). 49% of qualified picks read DISTRIBUTION at signal time, because Wyckoff events fire at high-volume pivot highs — structurally what a breakout looks like.

And what the journal does NOT say

What the journal is not

The −₹4.99L realized loss is not a verdict on this system. It is substantially tax-loss harvesting — 21 of ~25 harvest exits in a single FY-end batch — of random, discretionary picks that never came from the Catalyst/GM+S4 pipeline (ETFs and large-cap defensives). The journal mixes system and hand-picked trades and cannot be separated retroactively, so no clean live system track record exists yet. The only clean evidence is the backtest, and it is positive. Build the live record forward from trades carrying a true entry snapshot.

Part 11

The behavioural rules

The hardest ones — and the reason the rest exist.

Do

  • Act on the alert, not the touch. Watching a name tick-by-tick is how the confirmation rule gets broken.
  • Write the override down. Going against the system is allowed — it is your money and your read — but log it with the reason. After twenty rows the log tells you whether your instinct beats the system.
  • Log the passes. A log of entries only is a survivorship-biased diary.
  • Judge the fix, not the P&L, on the day you make it. A correct change that lowers the headline number is still correct.

Don't

  • Don't build instead of trading. The long-standing pattern is to build features when a trade needs taking. When the urge arrives mid-session, ask what you are avoiding.
  • Don't re-open a chart you already passed on. That is how a pass becomes a chase — one decision per name per session.
