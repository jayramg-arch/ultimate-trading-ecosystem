01The setup that cannot fire

S8 — Choppy Range requires the change-of-character count over the trailing twenty bars to reach three or more. Its condition is a single line, and it is exact.

Measured, on the component that feeds it

Across 38 board names, on daily and 75-minute, at swing lengths 5, 7 and 10 — that count never reached 3 in any cell. The distribution tops out at 2, which occurs on 3% of daily names and 11–13% of intraday ones.

So S8 has never labelled a bar, and its −1 penalty has never been applied. The “sit out” signal the checklist tells you to respect is not reachable — and the same threshold makes the Structure Health BROKEN tier unreachable for the same reason.

Three consequences worth holding:

ConsequenceWhat to do
The score's downside is narrower than documentedOnly S7 can push it down. The −1 rung is theoretical
“Never enter on a negative bonus” means only one thingIn practice it means never enter against S7
The chop warning does not existIts absence is not evidence the tape is clean. Judge chop from the structure-health count itself — 2 is as high as it goes

Left as-is deliberately rather than re-tiered: changing the threshold to make the labels look right would break parity with the sibling surface for a cosmetic gain, and there is still no evidence that a higher count predicts a worse trade. Change both surfaces together or neither.

02Architecture

Four modules produce component scores; those sum to a base; a detector reads the base and classifies the bar into at most one setup; the setup returns a signed bonus; base plus bonus is the final score, and the band comes from that.

Wyckoff  ─┐
Profile  ─┤
Zones    ─┼──> BASE SCORE ──┐
Stage    ─┘        │         ├──> FINAL SCORE ──> band ──> display

                   └──> Setup Detector ──> bonus ──┘

03Breaking the loop

“The setup uses the score, the score uses the setup” is circular. The design breaks it by strict ordering, enforced by code position rather than convention:

  1. The four components compute. That sum is the base score.
  2. The detector runs, reading only the base. Every threshold in a setup's conditions — base at least 6 for S2, base at most −3 for S7 — refers to the raw figure.
  3. The bonus is computed from the detected setup.
  4. Final score is base plus bonus.
  5. The band is assigned from the final.

A setup can never see its own bonus. That is the whole trick, and it is worth noticing that it is guaranteed by where the lines sit in the file — the bonus variable does not exist yet when the detector runs. Nothing enforces it at review time; the order does.

04The bullish five

Each is an AND-gate across all four modules. Folded, strongest first.

S2 · Spring or last-point-of-support reversal — priority 5, bonus +2

The maximum-conviction long. The textbook accumulation resolution: a false breakdown that recovered, or a successful retest of that low, followed by a structural flip and value-area support.

WyckoffMost recent event is a spring or a last point of support
FreshnessWithin 15 bars
StructureTrend bullish
ProfileScore at least +1 — price has demand-side acceptance
Base scoreAt least 6 — the composite must already be constructive

Note the base-score floor. S2 cannot rescue a weak chart — it only upgrades one that is already good. That is what makes the +2 defensible.

S3 · Sweep plus change of character — priority 4, bonus +2

The stop-run reversal. Price takes out a prior swing low, then breaks structure upward.

SweepA bullish liquidity sweep within 10 bars
StructureA bullish change of character within 10 bars
ProfileScore above −3 — a sweep at extreme distribution is a dead cat, not a reversal

The sweep removes hidden supply at the obvious low; the change of character confirms buyers took the structure. No base-score floor here — unlike S2, this one can fire on a weaker composite, which is why its entry is a pullback rather than the bar itself.

S1 · Order-block retest with profile support — priority 3, bonus +1

The bread-and-butter entry: price retesting a bullish block while still in accumulation and supported by the profile.

LocationPrice is inside a bullish order block
ProfileScore at least +1, or within 5% of the control point
WyckoffBias is accumulation
Base scoreAt least 3 — modest confirmation

The most repeatable of the five, and the one whose stop is most obvious: below the block's low.

S4 · Value-area-low bounce with a gap stack — priority 2, bonus 0
LocationThe low tagged the value-area low within 1% and the close is back above it
GapsMore unfilled bullish gaps than bearish
WyckoffNot distribution — accumulation or neutral both qualify
Base scoreAt least 2

Auction-theory mean reversion: a defended lower bound with buyers still aggressive above. Target the control point, then the value-area high.

S5 · Stage-2 continuation above value — priority 2, bonus 0
Stage2, advancing
StructureA bullish break — not a change of character — within 20 bars
ProfileClose above the value-area high: price has accepted above the whole prior range
CleanlinessChange-of-character count at most 1

That last gate is near-inert in practice. The count is 0 or 1 on the large majority of names, so it excludes only the 3–13% sitting at 2. It is not dead like S8 — but do not think of it as doing much filtering.

S6 · Sign-of-strength momentum — priority 2, bonus 0
WyckoffMost recent event is a sign of strength, within 10 bars
ProfileScore at least +1
StructureTrend bullish

Enter on the first shallow pullback and trail aggressively — these bars precede further markup, and they also precede climaxes, and the bar itself does not tell you which.

05The bearish two, and the dead one

S7 · Distribution breakdown — priority 3, bonus −2

The exit flag, and the mirror of S3.

WyckoffA sign of weakness, last point of supply, or upthrust — within 15 bars
StructureTrend bearish
LocationInside a bearish block, or profile score at most −1
Base scoreAt most −3

Four conditions aligned, exactly as S3 requires four — which is why the magnitude matches at two. Exit longs; the short is optional.

S8 · Choppy range — priority 1, bonus −1 · UNREACHABLE

Requires no other setup to have fired and a change-of-character count of at least 3. Section 01 — that count has never been observed above 2. This setup has never labelled a bar.

Documented here because the checklist and the sizing table both still reference it, and because knowing a rung is dead is different from not knowing it exists. Were it reachable, it would mean: no new positions, tighten what you hold, wait for one clean break to clear the chop.

06Priority, and the one genuine conflict

At most one setup labels a bar. Priority resolves the rest:

S2 (5)  >  S3 (4)  >  S1 (3) = S7 (3)  >  S4 = S5 = S6 (2)  >  S8 (1)  >  none

S7 is evaluated independently of the bull setups. It can override anything at priority 3 or below — S1, S4, S5, S6 — but it cannot override S2.

The S2-versus-S7 case

A spring has just printed and fresh distribution is visible. S2 wins on priority, so the panel shows a maximum-conviction long on a chart that is simultaneously showing exit-volume bars.

Treat that as a flag to read the chart by hand, not as a verdict. It is rare, and it is the one case where the priority ladder gives a confident answer to a genuinely ambiguous picture.

Why the priority-2 trio pays nothing, and this is the best decision in the design

They fire on conditions that already drove the component scores. S5 fires when stage is 2 and price is above the value area — stage is already at +3 and the profile at +3. S6 fires on a sign of strength, which already put Wyckoff at +4. S4 fires on a gap stack the zone score has already counted.

Paying a bonus for them would be counting the same evidence twice. The label still appears for situational awareness; the score stays honest. Most scoring systems get this wrong by rewarding every detector that fires.

The four score rows

RowShows

Showing the arithmetic is the point. A score of 8 built from base 6 plus an S2 bonus is a different signal from a pure base 8 with no setup — the first is one detector's opinion on top of a good chart, the second is four modules agreeing without needing one. The panel refuses to let you confuse them.

The full panel — 24 rows in five blocks

Read from the live table rather than the guide, which defers rows 0 to 18 to a version that is no longer shipped.

Six of these seven rows are literally the inputs to a setup condition. When a setup does not fire, this block tells you which condition missed — that is what it is for.

Rows 10–15 · Wyckoff and profile
RowWhat it carries
Wyckoff biasAccumulation, distribution or neutral — persists until another event fires
Last eventThe event name with its volume multiple, and its decayed score

VP levelsThe three prices themselves
Distance to control pointColoured asymmetrically — above scores friendlier than the same distance below

Bias does not decay but the score does, so a chart can read “accumulation” at a decayed score of zero — bullish, a long time ago. Read the two rows together or not at all.

Rows 17–18 · stage and setup
RowWhat it carries
Weinstein stageThe digit and its label, from the weekly 2×2
SetupThe detected setup with its prefix — a tick for bullish, a cross for bearish, a dot for the choppy label. The prefix is what the bonus logic actually reads

Worth knowing: the bonus is derived by testing the string prefix of the setup label rather than a numeric type. It works, and it means the label and the score can never disagree about direction — but it is also why the label text is not free to change.

Notice the hold periods run from two bars to six months across seven setups on one panel. The setup label is therefore also a trade-class label — reading S4 and holding it like S5 is a different trade from the one the system flagged.

The execution manual, setup by setup

Sizing assumes the standard 1% risk rule; the multiplier scales that 1%, it does not replace it. Every stop is volatility-aware — clamp it to at least one unit from entry so it survives normal noise, however tight the structure looks.

The universal rule people break first

Confirm the panel on the same timeframe you are entering on. Reading an S2 on the daily and entering off a fifteen-minute impulse is not that setup — the panel state is timeframe-specific, and so are every one of its thresholds.

S2 · execution
EntryA close above the spring or support-point pivot high on volume at 1.5× the recent average. On the retest variant, the first up-bar that holds above the prior swing low
StopBelow the swept low, less a quarter unit. Re-entering the swept zone means the spring failed — that is the structural invalidation, not a price you chose
First targetHalf off at the control point — typically 2 to 3R
SecondA quarter off at the value-area high, usually the next supply node
RunnerA quarter, trailed at two units or the short average, whichever is wider
ManagementBreakeven at 1R. After the first target, trail the most recent confirmed higher low the structure module prints
Force exitThe spring low breaks intrabar · a bearish change of character within 10 bars of entry · the bias flips to distribution
S3 · execution
EntryDo not chase the reversal bar. Wait for the pullback into the sweep candle's origin, or into the block the break created
StopBelow the sweep wick, less a quarter unit
First targetHalf off at the lower high the reversal broke — 2 to 3R
SecondThe next liquidity pool above
ManagementFaster than S2 — breakeven at 1.5R, half off at 2R, the rest on a two-unit trail
Force exitA bearish change of character · a close back below the sweep low · base score dropping below zero
S1 · execution
EntryA bullish reaction candle inside the block. The box is the entry zone, not a price
StopBelow the block's low, less a quarter unit — a close beneath it invalidates the block itself
TargetsHalf at the control point or value high, whichever is nearer; the rest at the prior swing high
ManagementBreakeven at 1R. If it stalls inside value for more than five bars without a higher high, leave at breakeven — the setup has timed out
Force exitBlock low broken on a close · bias off accumulation · base score below 3

The time-stop is the part people skip. A block that has not produced a higher low in five bars is being distributed into, not defended.

S4 · execution
EntryA bullish bar closing in its upper half with the low at or within 1% of the value-area low, on at least average volume
StopBelow the value-area low, less half a unit. It is a soft floor, so it needs more room than a block
Targets60% at the control point — the standard rotation — then the rest at the value-area high if it punches through with momentum
ManagementAt 1R, trail just below the most recent bullish gap that has been filled. The unfilled gaps above are your stepping stones
Force exitA close below the value low · more bearish gaps forming above than bullish · bias flips to distribution
S5 · execution
EntryEither a three-bar low back into the prior value high, now support — or an add on the next structural break above
StopBelow that prior value high, less half a unit
TargetsA third at 1.5R to fund the runner; a third at the measured move — the value area's own width projected up
RunnerA third, trailed on the weekly 30-week average
ManagementResist tightening. This is a trend-follow; let normal pullbacks breathe. Exit the runner only on a weekly stage-3 print or a close below the anchor
Force exitClose below the 30-week · stage flips to topping · a bearish change of character on the entry timeframe

The only setup here whose runner is managed on a weekly clock. Hold is six weeks to six months — this is where the positional book actually lives.

S6 · execution
EntryNot the strength bar itself — it is already extended. Wait for the first two-to-three bar pullback holding above that bar's midpoint, then enter on the next up-close
StopBelow the strength bar's low, less a quarter unit. Violate it and the whole momentum thesis is gone
TargetsHalf at 1.5 to 2R; the rest on a three-bar trailing low — deliberately aggressive
ManagementWatch for a buying climax or an upthrust on the panel. Both are exit signals before the stop is reached. Reduce if the Wyckoff event turns
Force exitA climax or upthrust prints · close below the strength bar's midpoint · the bias decays out of accumulation

The tightest management of the six, because a sign of strength precedes a further leg and a climax about equally often — and the bar cannot tell you which.

S7 · execution, and the NSE caveat
Holding a longExit immediately. No waiting. Four independent conditions confirmed the distribution — the burden of proof has moved
Short entryThe first pullback into the distribution bar or the nearest bearish block
StopAbove that bar's high, plus half a unit. On an upthrust specifically, above the wick
TargetsThe control point if price was above value, the value low if it was rejected at the high; then the prior swing low or the 30-week
Force exitA bullish change of character · a close back above the distribution high · base score recovering above zero

Indian retail cannot short equity beyond intraday. Treat S7 as an exit and no-buy signal, not a short entry, unless you are trading derivatives. Everything in the short column above is conditional on a mandate most readers of this page do not have.

S8 · what it would have meant

Recorded for completeness — this cannot fire, per section 01.

ActionNo new entries, full stop

11How setups chain

A clean advance produces a sequence, and watching for the sequence beats watching for any single event:

accumulation resolves   S2   first entry
markup begins           S6   add on the first shallow pullback
markup continues        S1   add on each clean retest of a new block
trend matures           S5   the trailing position; ride it
distribution appears    S7   exit
And the failure tell

S2 followed by S8 within ten bars means the spring was a trap — exit immediately rather than waiting for the stop.

Except that S8 cannot fire, so this tell cannot trigger. If you want it, watch the structure-health count going to 2 shortly after an S2 instead — that is the reachable version of the same warning.

12What the bonus actually changes

Four worked cases. The useful finding is that the band moves in only two of them — most of the value is attribution, not reclassification.

CaseBaseBonusFinalBand moves?
Mid-strength stage 2, fresh S29+211No — strong bull either way. But the panel now shows why
Modest composite, S3 fires6+28No — bull either way, because the top threshold moved up with the bonus
Weak composite, S7 fires−5−2−7Yes — caution becomes bear. The warning escalates
Borderline bull, S8 fires4−13Would demote bull to neutral — but S8 cannot fire
Read the second row carefully

Raising the strong-bull threshold by one to make room for the bonus means a +2 bonus frequently buys no band change at all — it moved the goalposts by roughly what it adds. The bonus is therefore doing most of its work as visible attribution, and only occasionally as reclassification.

That is defensible, and it is not what the version notes imply. The one place it genuinely escalates is the bearish side, where the threshold did not move.

Loading conflict

This layer replaces the three standalone modules. Running it alongside them double-draws every zone and every level — and the standalone files no longer exist, so if you have them on a chart they are stale copies.

On the catalyst mapping

Each setup has a rough screener-catalyst equivalent — the spring maps to the positional breakout and accumulation labels, the sweep reversal to the swing ones, distribution to the recovery family. The mapping is informational only. The screener computes its catalysts independently from price and volume; nothing here feeds it, and a disagreement between the two is information rather than a fault.
