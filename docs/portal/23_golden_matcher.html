01Two surfaces, one engine

Where the day is spent

The Trigger Board

Answers which of ~50 should I even look at. One row per qualified name, sorted by how close it is to firing.

When a name earns a look

Single Symbol

Answers is this one a buy, and what is my next physical action. A gated six-step path with a verdict at the top.

They agree by construction, not by luck

Both surfaces call one evaluator. When they disagreed in the past it was never two engines drifting — it was a symbol-normalisation slip, a different timeframe, or one view holding fresher data than the other. All three are now closed, and both views share a single Refresh.

ETFs and indices — PHASE, not Stage

An ETF row carries its index for structure and the ETF for the trigger. Those two steps were called Stage 1 and Stage 2 until 1 September, which collided with the Weinstein stage printed three rows away on the same screen. They are now PHASE 1 and PHASE 2; the watchlist emits each index adjacent to its ETF so the pair stays together however you sort.

Put the board on the second monitor and keep TradingView on the primary. The board narrows; the chart decides. After any code change, restart the app — a browser reload does not reload Python.

02Inherited qualification

The watchlists QUALIFY. The board TIMES.

A name arriving from a rigorous overnight scan has already been qualified on technicals and fundamentals. Re-running that screen on the board throws the work away — and it did exactly that: only the broad catalyst scan produced actionable output, while the most rigorous lists dead-ended at “no catalyst” or “weak fundamentals”.

So each name carries the archetype of the list that qualified it — breakout, pullback, leader, catalyst-scan, at-value, recovery, or a pyramid add on something already held — and the board reports only its timing state. Three consequences:

  • Context and quality stop being vetoes and become a still-valid guard: a name is invalidated only on an observed break-down — Stage 3/4, or price below the 30-week average. Missing data never invalidates; only an observed break does.
  • Fundamentals rank rather than block. This is what unblocked the recovery side, whose fast-mode fundamental read was rejecting everything.
  • The inherited archetype is the setup, so no live catalyst is required.
The exception that keeps it honest

A name qualified only by the broad catalyst scan still needs a live catalyst or a fired pattern — otherwise it reads catalyst expired. A snapshot-based archetype should not outlive the snapshot that produced it.

04S4-GO — the complete grammar

This is the single most-read cell on the board, and it carries seven kinds of suffix. Two of them look superficially alike and mean opposite things. Read the glyph, not the letter.

The stem — the gate count, which is what the column sorts on

StemMeaning
5/5 GOAll five gates align on the live bar
5/5 · PA NbThe other four are true now; the pattern fired N bars ago (≤3). Still actionable — but the entry anchors to the bar that fired, which is exactly what Section 4's latch does
4/5 · no volArmed, at a location, clean bar, books fine — waiting on volume
4/5 · weak barEverything but the bar — it closed red in its lower half
4/5 · no fundaFour technical gates pass and the books sit under the floor. Rare on the board, because a rejected name is normally suppressed to ⛔ funda before it reaches here
3/5 · no PALocation, volume and bar are fine; no pattern
2/5 · no locNeeds a pullback to a location
⛔ Stage NStage 3 or 4. Sorts below every live count. The gates may well all be true — a topping chart can fire a pattern at a location on volume — but the stage is upstream of all four
n/aNo usable read. “No data”, not “not attempted”

The five gates are: a pattern fired · price is at a location · relative volume clears the floor · the bar itself is clean · the books clear the floor. An unknown bar reading counts as a pass — the board never penalises a missing value.

Fundamentals are counted AND applied — and the two do different jobs

Since 2 September the fifth gate is in the count, so the board says 5/5 where the chart shows five chips. It was always applied: when the floor rejects a name the whole cell still becomes ⛔ funda and no count is shown, because 5/5 is the most eye-catching thing on a glance surface and advertising it on a rejected name invites exactly the trade the floor exists to prevent. What counting added is the third state — a name that was never scored passes the gate and carries F?, so it is no longer indistinguishable from a verified one.

So a 5/5 without F? has cleared fundamentals on a real score. Read it as worth opening rather than will fire for the ordinary reasons — a location that decays, a different feed, the board leading by design — not because a hidden gate is waiting.

The verdict the chart applies is the board's own, pasted across in the bundle. A blank F on the chart therefore means the paste is missing or stale, not that the two disagree about the company. Since 8 September a holding that is not on the board still carries its fundamentals into the bundle, so the chart can score a name you own even when no watchlist qualified it today.

The suffixes — every one is display-only and never changes the count

· PB

Pullback context

The relaxed gate applied — the volume floor dropped and the bar test became held the zone rather than closed strong. Fires only when a contraction pattern fired with no expansion pattern and price sits inside a demand zone. This is the setup you are hunting; PB 5/5s sort above breakout 5/5s.

· F?

Unscored books

The fundamental gate passed by default because no score exists for the name — the board never fetched one, or the source returned nothing. Not a fail and not a pass on evidence. The chart will show a blank F for the same reason.

· ↑D

HTF nesting — up arrow · POSITIVE

Price is held by a demand zone on a timeframe above the tab you are looking at. Appears only when the location gate already passed, and only for higher timeframes — so a daily zone shows on the 75m and 125m tabs and disappears on the Daily tab, where it is native.

· ⧖D

Daily fallback — hourglass · A FAULT

The intraday read failed, and the pattern behind this verdict came from the daily battery while the row still wears its intraday label. Ignore its verdict and rebuild. Distinct from ↑D in every way except the letter.

· N⚖

Knife-edge

N fired patterns sit on their threshold — they flip on a difference smaller than the routine gap between two data feeds. One name read Σ6 on the board and Σ2 on the chart for the same bar. A marginal Σ is not conviction.

· ⚠role

Role mismatch

The fired battery is ignition-only — expansion away from value — while price sits inside a demand zone, which is absorption at value. Both are arithmetically true of one bar while describing opposite things. It tags and never gates.

· ⚠noedge

Measured — no edge

On every recovery-path row. Was ⚠unval until 20 Sep; the recovery re-baseline has since reported (run 20260810_153105, 24 months, correctly windowed): mean matched alpha −0.88%, CI95 [−1.51, −0.24], no family positive in both windows. Roughly a fifth of the board. Tradeable on your own read through the reviewer; it is a measured non-edge, not an unmeasured one.

↑D is not ⧖D

The up-arrow is a strength term. The hourglass is a data fault. To check a whole tab at once, count the hourglasses in that tab's cache file — zero is the expected reading.

A board 4/4 is arming, not the verdict

The board's location read is the GM's twin of Section 4's, not the same code — it merges the zone engine, an order-block and pivot proxy, S/R levels and anchored VWAPs, while S4 on the chart uses its own engine and is the plan of record. Expect the occasional disagreement; when it happens, the chart wins.

Since 11 September — the board feeds the reviewer

python s4_review.py --board 75m takes every N/N GO row of that tab's cache — add --live for every Buy Trigger Live row too, which is how the recovery names (typically 3/5 · no vol, the normal recovery shape) get read — switches the TradingView charts to the name, reads S4 and Section Five off the canvas, and writes a deliberated TAKE / WAIT / PASS per name with the R-arithmetic, the OI state and (for ETFs) the index gate checked by the script. It warns when the cache is more than six hours old — rebuild before you run it. Since 16 September the S4 GO alerts do this on their own through a webhook, and every review is a page: Doc 31, The Reviewer Log; the manual is Doc 32.

04bThe handoff — two pastes, two fields

Section Four cannot see what qualified a name, cannot reach a fundamentals provider, and cannot read an option chain. So the board hands all three across as text, and since 1 September that is two pasted fields rather than one.

FieldCarriesRefresh
ONE-PASTE bundlePath resolution, pullback context, BFF / RFF / Piotroski and their component bitstrings, ETF liquidity and premium, board rank Every watchlist rebuild
Bundle 2 — options OIPCR, max pain, total CE/PE OI, the ATM shift and the two writer strikes, packed six-per-symbol for F&O names only Every Evening run (window or Phase 12); its button remains for a rebuild
They are not interchangeable

Pasting bundle 2 into the first field wipes every list in it. Two fields exist because the options data was added after the first bundle had settled, and keeping it separate means a missed second paste cannot blank the first. The "4,096-character cap" that used to be given as the reason is false — measured 20 Sep: S4's header receipt read 6943ch·15/14, every section received. The split stays as a convenience, not a constraint.

The options bundle is built by the Evening run, and otherwise sits behind a button rather than building with the board, because it costs one broker call per F&O name at one call every three seconds. Cash-only names are filtered out first from the scrip master with no network at all — on a 75-name board that is 52 names skipped and roughly three minutes saved. Expect around two and a half minutes and a bundle near 1,100 characters for ~23 F&O names.

Why the chain comes from the broker now

The exchange serves an option chain only around market hours and its index endpoint returns a hard 404 — so index chains had been failing permanently, not intermittently. The broker answers after hours, is authenticated rather than cookie-scraped, and is already the primary market-data source. The exchange remains as a fallback.

A field that could not be read is emitted blank, never zero. Three of these become drawn price levels on the chart, and a max pain of zero renders as a confident line below every stop that looks exactly like a real one.

What you do in the evening now

Open the Golden Matcher window — the sidebar entry opens it in its own window since 13 September, so the main window stays on Risk Shield — and copy the two blocks under the build stamp: ONE-PASTE bundle (all timeframes) and Bundle 2 — options OI. Paste them into S4. That is the whole ritual. The per-timeframe bundle and the individual lists are folded into a collapsed hand overrides expander; you will not need them.

Bundle 2 survives a restart now — it used to live only in session memory and vanish; it is read back from the file with the run's stamp.

Three tabs, three lists

The Trigger-TF selector loads that timeframe's cache on a switch (since 13 Sep — it used to show the last-built frame with a stale-snapshot warning). The row set is the same universe on all three; what differs is Category, S4-GO, PA and the plan. The title carries a colour chip — 75m green · 125m amber · Daily blue — so three windows are tellable apart. The warning now appears only when no cache exists for that timeframe, e.g. Monday morning against Friday's.

05Refresh cadence

Run the board on 75m + 125m bar-close. It then rebuilds once per session bar — 10:30 · 11:20 · 11:45 · 13:00 · 13:25 · 14:15 · 15:30, plus a short settle.

Why bar-close and not continuous

A board built mid-bar shows patterns that evaporate by the close. That is precisely the disagreement that used to look like a bug: the board read Buy Trigger Live while the single view said catalyst expired. Neither was wrong — they were reading different bars.

Three buttons — press one

Rebuild board reuses cached data and is fast; it is the right button after changing the X-Ray toggle. Fetch fresh data + rebuild invalidates the universe and rebuilds itself for this timeframe — pressing both just rebuilds twice on identical data. Evening run does the fetch once and all three timeframes plus the options bundle; after the close it is the only one you need.

Background tabs go stale: the refresh runs on a browser timer that the browser throttles when the tab is hidden. They catch up a few seconds after you focus them.

06The Armed Register — the board's memory

The failure it exists to prevent

You arm a name on Monday and set the alert. Tuesday the pipeline rebuilds the watchlists and the name drops out of all of them. Thursday the alert fires — and there is no row, no entry, no stop, no thesis. Only a ping and a chart.

The register follows the same doctrine one level up: the register qualifies, the board times. An armed name is injected into the union carrying the archetypes it had when armed, so it keeps its original path and is re-evaluated live on every rebuild.

What is stored is the plan as of arming — trigger, entry, stop, target, R:R, verdict, path, timeframe. That is the part you cannot reconstruct later, because those levels came off that day's bar. Live re-evaluation is what the board already does.

ActionEffect
ArmRecords the plan from that row; the name survives watchlist churn
DisarmKept as cancelled, not deleted
FilledMarked triggered; leaves the active set
ExpiryAutomatic at 45 days, applied on every read — an expiry that only runs on a button press never runs

The register panel shows the armed plan beside the live category and gate count, how long you have waited, and a warning on exactly the names the register is saving. That comparison — armed at X, now Y — is the thing to read when an alert fires.

The habit

Arm at the same moment you create the alert. They are a pair. An alert without a register entry is precisely the failure this was built for.

07The Pullback Finder — the “where is value” sibling

A trigger is a wide-range up-bar near the recent high, so a trigger-based board is breakout-biased by construction. That is not a bug in a gate — it is what a stopwatch does. Measured on a live board:

Timing statenMedian extension from EMA20At value
Buy Trigger Live81.74 ATR1 of 8
Armed Wait91.35 ATR4 of 9
Wait for Pullback170.23 ATR14 of 17

Every actionable name sat within a few percent of its own 20-day high — and the names actually at value were the ones labelled “wait”.

So the Pullback Finder ranks by location rather than trigger: extension from the EMA20 in ATR, pullback depth off the 20-day high, volume dry-up, range contraction, and the nearest real support below price. Hard gates are Stage 2, above a rising 200-day, positive relative strength, and no climax volume bar. It scans the full universe and skips the fundamental join — the two things that were starving pullback supply.

Trigger> in its output is the confirmation level, not an entry. Wait for a closed bar above it, then buy-stop above that bar.

08The six-step path

A vertical, gated sequence with a verdict header. Each step shows metric chips, an imperative guidance line, a status pill, and a ← NOW badge on the step that needs you. The two hard gates can stop the path outright: a hard fail greys out every step below it, so the sequence will not let you skip to a trigger on a name that failed context or quality.

Why the two paths show different RS numbers for the same stock

They are different measures, not a bug: the bull path shows the RRG ratio convention, the recovery path shows Mansfield RS on a different scale. Compare each to its own path.

What LOCATION actually accepts — three states and two kinds of evidence

Price does not have to be inside a zone. IN and REACTING (tested once, turning up off it, and not yet two ATR away) both satisfy the gate; APPROACHING is named on the board and deliberately does not — nothing has reacted yet, so honouring it would be buying the touch.

Rule A2 governs the kind. A pattern zone stands alone; a pivot shelf must be confirmed by an S/R level or an anchored VWAP. Pivot levels — a pivot line, or a "Pivot S→R" once price has reclaimed it — count as pivot evidence and inherit the same requirement. Turning pivots off in settings removes them from zones, location, the drawn lines and the overhead used by Room; that last part flatters R:R, so it is a deliberate trade rather than a free tightening.

Measured, and it did not settle the question: a pre-registered A/B over eighteen anchors put any-zone, A2 and pattern-only within 0.21 percentage points of each other on matched alpha — a null — while the trade count fell 221 → 88 → 51. Pattern-only is rarer, not better. A2 ships because the evidence cannot separate them.

10The guided execution

At Step 5 the page produces a position size from your capital and risk percentage, halves it automatically on counter-trend names, and — when an order block, gap or pivot support sits under price — auto-fills the first two checklist steps with that zone and its proximal, so you verify rather than hand-draw.

  1. Mark the fresh demand zone on daily or above — or verify the auto-detected one is genuinely fresh and untested.
  2. Set the alert at the zone proximal. Arm the name at the same moment.
  3. Wait for a 75m or 125m bar to CLOSE in your direction at the zone.
  4. Place a buy-stop above that trigger bar's high. Never buy the touch.
  5. Stop below the zone distal, sized at your risk percentage.
  6. Place the order and the GTT the same evening, and log the trade.

Tick all six and a journal form appears, pre-filled from the active plan. Logging it auto-captures the true entry snapshot, so the trade lands in the attribution pipeline carrying its setup label — which is the only way a system-only track record ever gets built.

The session shortlist

Every actionable name you land on accumulates into a ranked list you can export straight to a TradingView watchlist. A name that later degrades drops off by itself. It turns a scrolling session into an artifact.

11The walkthrough

  1. Read the header verdict first. If it says AVOID, stop and go to the next name.
  2. Context. Not a leader → skip. No daily setup rescues a Stage 3/4 or RS-negative name.
  3. Quality. Fail → watchlist, revisit when it firms up.
  4. Setup. No live catalyst → buy-watch: alert at the zone, wait.
  5. Location. Extended or below value → wait for pullback. Do not chase. A green support chip is the confluence you want before Step 5.
  6. Trigger. Fired → note which pattern and its tier. A high tier is a strong trigger; a lone coil means wait for the release. Not fired → set the alert and walk away.
  7. Switch to the chart. Section 4 on the name, drop to 75m or 125m, wait for the GO. The board arms; the chart executes.
The one rule, again

The Golden Matcher identifies. You trigger. Never buy because price arrived at the zone. The whole page is built to stop you buying the touch.

Why the match is 5/5 and not GO — measured

The cell reads 5/5 GO only when the pattern fired on the live bar; a few bars old it reads 5/5 · PA 3b, with no “GO” in the string. Matching on GO therefore hid names where all gates pass but the pattern is not fresh — one such name was worth a full review. When this was measured under the four-gate count, matching GO kept 4 of 49 rows; matching the count kept 10.

The match is anchored to the start of the string on purpose: the upstream vetoes render as ⛔ Stage 3 · gates 5/5, which contain 5/5 — anchoring keeps them out.

The rotation tag — display only, and the bug behind it

A row carrying the rotation tag is one whose trajectory is not on the tradeable whitelist. It never vetoes and never changes the gate count.

The board computes it itself rather than reading a column, and that detail matters: only one of the source files carries the pre-computed flag. The main file — 49 of 51 board rows — does not, so a column-based gate would have failed open on about 96% of the board while looking shipped.

A bug worth remembering as a class

The first version returned nothing for every symbol. The stock's weekly bars came from one resampler and the benchmark's from a native weekly fetch — the two anchor differently, so the join dropped every row and the gate failed open silently.

If you ever add a weekly comparison: resample both sides the same way, or verify the join is non-empty. A gate that fails open looks exactly like a gate that passes.

The veto is switched off deliberately. Before restoring it, read the gate section in the Section Four guide — the whitelist it would apply measured +0.00pp at a twelve-week horizon.

Why Stage, RS and rotation numbers all moved — and by how much

Three corrections landed in the producer that feeds every board column. None changed which names fire — catalyst selection was unchanged across all 49 rows — but they changed the context those names are shown with.

1 · The rotation engine was recalibrated

The producer was still running an older lookback pair, so it disagreed with every other surface. It now imports the shared calibration rather than copying it, and was verified identical to the reference engine to twelve decimal places across thousands of weekly observations. Every surface now runs the same maths.

2 · Weekly indicators no longer use the forming week

Found because two panels on one screen disagreed — one card read leading, another read weakening. Neither was stale and neither had drifted: one used confirmed weekly bars, the other included the week in progress. On a Tuesday that “weekly” bar holds two sessions.

The forming bar is now dropped from both the stock and the benchmark leg, and it reads the pinned date when replaying, so a historical run drops the week that was forming at that anchor. Blast radius across 49 names: stage changed on 5, quadrant on 5, tradeable on 3, catalyst on none.

3 · What that means when you read the board

Values shifted for roughly one name in ten — two moved up a stage and became blocked, one moved down and was admitted. That is a correction, not a regression — but do not compare today's Overall against a screenshot from last week.

Single Symbol — page anatomy

The header strip

Symbol and company with its sector, the current price and change, and a freshness line — which reads green when the last bar is current and warns when it lags a completed session. On load a stale frame auto-heals once per session: the page busts its cache and reloads to pull the fresh bar. If the provider genuinely has not published it, the warning stays. Then the catalyst, large and colour-coded — this is the screener's setup label, not the pattern trigger.

The decision path

Each step shows metric chips, an imperative guidance line, a status pill, and a ← NOW badge on the step that needs you. A hard fail greys out everything below it.

The trigger step

Names which patterns fired and their combined tier. Patterns are bonuses, not a checklist — you read the tier. A high tier or a strong single pattern is a real trigger; a lone coil means wait for the release.

Guided execution

A position sizer from your capital and risk percentage, automatically halved on counter-trend names. When a zone sits under price the first two checklist items are auto-filled with that zone and its proximal, so you verify rather than hand-draw. Tick all six and a journal form appears pre-filled — logging it captures the true entry snapshot, which is the only way a system-only track record ever gets built.

Session shortlist and full metrics

Every actionable name you land on accumulates into a ranked list, exportable straight to a watchlist; a name that later degrades drops off by itself. The full-metrics expander holds a score strip and ten detail cards — the strip exists so you only open a card when its score surprises you.

Two verdict engines, and why

The decision path is your working view. A compact three-gate banner inside the expander is the cross-check. Both speak the same trigger language, and the banner's displayed sub-checks were aligned to the thresholds it actually enforces — so a gate never shows a tick over red criteria.

26 Aug — why the Daily tab used to disagree with 75m and 125m

The whole strict-location block — rule A2, the strict flag, and every loc_* field — sat inside a branch that only ran for 75m and 125m. So the Daily tab silently kept the old saturated gate: 19 "Buy Trigger Live" against 13 on 75m, names scoring 4/4 while sitting 11% below their nearest zone, and a blank Loc column because the keys it reads were never set.

It presented as a data problem and was a scoping one. The fix was to extract the rule into one function called on both paths rather than copy it — a second copy is how the two drifted in the first place. Daily now passes location on 47% of names with a 9-pattern / 9-pivot split, the same shape as the intraday tabs.
