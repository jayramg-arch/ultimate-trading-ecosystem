02The targets moved, and the guide did not

Do not size a trade off the guide's matrix

The v2 guide's catalyst table gives positional targets of 5R / 10R / 15R and swing 3R / 5R / 8R. The live file computes 3R / 5R / 8R positional and 2R / 4R / 6R swing.

FamilyGuide T1Live T1Guide T2Live T2
Positional · Wyckoff · recovery5R3R10R5R
Swing3R2R5R4R

The code is correct and the guide is stale. The live values are the R-canon that runs everywhere else in the system — swing 2R/4R, positional 3R/5R, nothing under 2R — and they exist because at the old 5R the first target was reached on about 2% of positional trades. The partial never filled, the stop never moved to breakeven, and the trail never engaged. A target nobody reaches is not a conservative target; it is an absent one.

The stretch third leg at 8R / 6R is allocator-specific and has no counterpart in the strategy — it is a runner objective, not part of the canon.

03The auto-detect fallback does not do what the guide says

The guide states that when nothing matches, “the panel uses your manual selector” and the source row reads auto found none, using manual.

The code does that only when both context tests fail. If either passes, it assigns a family regardless of whether any catalyst was detected:

if positional-context and swing-context   → the detected catalyst, else "POS"
else if positional-context               → a positional catalyst, else "POS"
else if swing-context                    → an SWG-prefixed catalyst, else "SWG"
Half-closed, 25 Aug 2026 — the label no longer lies

The fallback is unchanged, but it no longer reports itself as a detection. AUTO now means a catalyst was actually detected; CTX means none fired and the family came from context; MAN means you set it. The payload carries whichever applies, so the ambiguity stops travelling downstream.

Why this matters more than it looks

The source flag is set to AUTO whenever the family is not “none” — and after those fallbacks it almost never is. So the panel reads AUTO on a name where nothing was detected, and the label means “a family was assigned” rather than “a catalyst was found”.

That assignment is not cosmetic. It picks the targets (3R/5R against 2R/4R), the trail (4.5× against 1.5×) and the stop fallback (4.0× ATR against 1.5×). A trending name with no identifiable setup is silently planned as a swing trade — and a merely-stage-2 name as a positional one.

The context tests themselves are reasonable — the swing one wants price above a rising short average above the 50-day, with momentum or a volume expansion, and not extended more than 15%. The problem is only that passing a context test is reported as detecting a catalyst.

This is on the open list as the “SWG fallback” item. Until it is resolved, treat the AUTO tag as a family assignment and check the setup yourself — or turn auto-detect off, where the manual selector is honest about being manual.

04Risk-first sizing

The whole tool inverts the usual order. You do not decide a quantity and discover your risk — you fix the risk and the quantity falls out.

risk amount   = capital × adjusted risk %
quantity      = risk amount ÷ (entry − stop)
                then capped by the maximum allocation for that asset class

Two consequences worth stating plainly:

A wider stop buys a smaller positionAutomatically, with no judgement involved. This is why the stop must be structural — move it for comfort and you have silently resized the trade
The allocation cap can bind before the risk math doesWhen both quantity columns show the same number, the cap is what set your size, not the risk calculation

Capital and risk are held per asset class — a separate base and a separate percentage for funds and for stocks, each with its own ceiling. Set once.

05The catalyst matrix

One family drives four things. Read the row for the family you are trading:

FamilyStructural stopStop fallbackTrailT1 · T2 · T3
Positional10-bar low, less 0.2 units4.0×Chandelier 4.5×3R · 5R · 8R
WyckoffBase low × 0.983.5×Chandelier 3.5×3R · max(5R, 52wH) · 8R
Recovery20-bar low, less 0.5 units2.5×Chandelier 2.5×3R · the 52-week high · that high plus 5%
SwingThe lower of the short average and the 10-bar low, less 0.11.5×Short average, about 1.5×2R · 4R · 6R

The fallback multiple applies when the structural anchor comes out at or above the entry — which happens more often than you would expect on a name that has already run.

Recovery targets are structural, not multiples

The second target for a recovery trade is the 52-week high itself, and Wyckoff takes whichever is higher of its multiple and that high. That is deliberate: on a beaten-down name the prior high is a real destination, and an R-multiple measured off a wide recovery stop can land somewhere meaningless.

In a weak tape every trail widens by half a unit — the same regime adjustment the Chandelier applies everywhere else.

06The three risk adjustments

Base risk is adjusted by three independent multipliers before the quantity is computed.

AdjustmentHow it works
ConvictionThree points — stage 2, positive strength, volume above average. Three points scales risk up 25%; two leaves it; one or none cuts it 25%
VolatilityAbove 3% daily range, risk scales down in proportion. A wilder name gets less capital at the same stop distance
RegimeA quarter point off the base in a weak tape

Two floors keep it sane: the combined volatility-and-conviction multiplier cannot fall below 0.75, and the final risk cannot fall below 0.25%. The tool will shrink a position; it will not refuse to size one.

Reading the adjusted figure

Adjusted equal to base means nothing objected. Adjusted below base is the market telling you something — usually volatility, sometimes conviction — and the honest response is to take the smaller size rather than override it. Adjusted above base means all three conviction points landed, and it is the only case where the tool asks for more than your standard risk.

07Legs and quantities

Updated 25 Aug 2026. Both target legs are now derived from the family — they mirror bull_screener.partial_qty_for, the same split the backtester and Risk Shield use. T2 exit % and T3 exit % became overrides: 0 means “use the house canon”.

Previously T2 was a flat input default of 25 for every catalyst, so a swing trade took positional quantities against swing R-multiples — the same paired-fallback defect partial_qty_for was hoisted on the Python side to fix.

FamilyT1 shareRationale
Positional · Wyckoff · recovery25 / 25Leaves a 50% runner. Not a preference: 88% of positional trades exit on the trail and essentially none on a target, so the untargeted half is where the return is
Swing (BO / PB)33 / 33A shorter horizon earns a bigger first take
Gap · reversion50 / 50Gaps fade and reversions are not runners — take it off
T3 now defaults to 0 — and here is why it did harm

There is no third leg in house policy: it is two target legs and a runner. Only three orders reach the broker anyway, so a 25% T3 was a chart-only number that still cut the untargeted runner from 50% to 25%. Set it deliberately if you want a stretch target.

The split mode still defaults to 50/50, and that costs the runner

Two OCO legs at 50% cover the whole position, so there is no runner — and it changes the payoff: T1 pays on half the shares while the stop costs on all of them, making a 2R first target roughly 1:1 in practice rather than 2:1. Left as the default because it is a Dhan constraint, not a preference; the tooltip now says what it costs.

Four legs here, three orders at the broker

The allocator plans T1, T2, T3 and a runner. What actually gets placed is two paired exits plus one standalone stop covering the uncapped tail — three orders, not four.

So the third target is a plan for the runner rather than an order you will rest. Decide before you place whether T3 is a real leg or a note to yourself, because the quantities do not map one-to-one onto what the broker will hold.

08The workflow

  1. Have the setup first. This is a sizer, not a scanner — it plans a trade you have already decided to take.
  2. Set capital and risk once, per asset class.
  3. Click the entry. That is the only mandatory click; the stop and first target fill themselves from the family.
  4. Override the stop only for structure — a level the chart gives you, not a number that makes the quantity look better.
  5. Read the panel top to bottom and check the validation row before anything else.
The validation row is the one people skim

It warns when the first target sits above overhead resistance — the 52-week high or the 200-day. That is not a detail. It means the plan's reward assumes price walks through a level it has historically been rejected at, and the reward-to-risk you are about to accept is fictional.

A zero quantity is not a failure — it means the risk amount cannot buy a single share at that stop distance. Widen nothing; the trade is too big for the account at that stop.

The rotation row, and what to do with it

Two numbers — the strength value and its four-week rate of change — resolve to a quadrant.

QuadrantReadingMeaning
LeadingBoth positiveOutperforming and accelerating. The quadrant to be long in
WeakeningValue positive, momentum notStill ahead, but the lead is fading
ImprovingValue negative, momentum positiveBehind, but recovering. Early-stage
LaggingBoth negativeBehind and deteriorating
RuleDetail
The floor for a longImproving or better. Do not open in lagging
The best cellLeading with strong momentum — these are where the conviction multiplier reaches its maximum and the full risk budget is deployed
The alert while heldLeading to weakening is a management signal, not an exit. Tighten the trail, or take the first partial even short of the target

The positional context test uses the same quadrant, and it excludes only lagging — so a name can pass into a positional plan while merely improving. The rotation row is where you see that.

Two operational details

Futures and optionsSet the lot multiplier to the contract size and the quantity row becomes lots rather than shares — the invested and risk figures scale with it. Leave it at one for cash equity
Resetting a planThe clicked prices persist until cleared. Zero the fields in the settings to re-run the click sequence — or remove and re-add from a template, which is faster and guarantees a clean slate

The persistence is the trap: switch symbols and the previous name's entry, stop and target are still loaded, quietly sizing a completely different trade. Same limitation the markup engine carries, for the same reason — inputs do not follow the symbol.

10Export

Two one-shot toggles: one sends the plan as a note, the other emits a structured payload for the order tooling — entry, stop, three legs with quantities, the runner, and the trail specification.

Both work the same way: tick, confirm, untick. They fire on the tick, not continuously.

The payload carries the catalyst source field. Given section 03, a payload can reach the order tooling stamped AUTO for a name where nothing was detected — so the label travels downstream with the same ambiguity it has on the panel.
