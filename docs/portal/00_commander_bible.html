What this document is, and what it is not

This is the system map: what each part of the ecosystem is for, why it exists, and how the parts hand work to each other. It is the document to read when you want the whole shape in one place, or when you come back after time away.

It deliberately does not restate the things that have their own page and would immediately drift here. For the per-trade gates read 18 · The Trade Funnel; for the clock-driven day, 26 · The Operating Loop; for what to do and not do when shortlisting, 25 · Golden Rules; for what the evidence supports, 27 · The Backtest Court.

Rewritten — what the previous version got wrong

The earlier Bible described a five-phase Python-and-TradingView workflow written before GM + S4 existed. That framing is retired. The system now has a ten-phase overnight pipeline, a timing board, a dedicated execution layer, a written pre-trade gate and a reconciled journal — a different machine, not a bigger version of the old one.

Three specific corrections carried over: POS-ACCUM is enabled, not disabled — it is the strongest catalyst measured. Indicator gates (RSI, ADX, MACD) are now pure price action. And the −₹4.99L journal figure is not a verdict on the system — it is largely tax-loss-harvested discretionary picks.

Section 1Philosophy — three schools, one edge

Stan Weinstein — stage analysis, the primary framework. Every stock is always in one of four stages. Stage 2 — under accumulation, trending up through its 30-week SMA — is the only stage where you want to own one. The 30-week average is the watershed, and stage transitions are the triggers. This is why Stage is computed identically on three surfaces and why a Stage 3 or 4 reading ends the conversation.

Mark Minervini — SEPA and the trend template, the entry filter. Within Stage 2, the trend template filters for the strongest names, and VCP breakouts and pullbacks give the low-risk entries. The Alpha Score puts a number on it.

Wyckoff, SMC and Volume Profile — the context layer. Institutional footprints confirm that money is genuinely accumulating rather than distributing.

The core discipline

Never trade against macro. Bear index or a Stage 3/4 sector means no long setups exist, however good the stock looks. Macro kills more trades than any technical error.

Confirmation before entry. Alert at the level, wait for a closed bar, then act. This is the single most valuable habit in the system and the one most often skipped.

Know the stop before the size. Size falls out of risk budget ÷ stop distance. If the answer is zero, the trade does not happen.

An override is allowed. An uncounted override is not. Write the sentence, log it.

Section 2The four jobs

Everything in the ecosystem is doing one of four jobs, and most confusion comes from asking a tool to do a job that belongs to another one.

QUALIFY

The watchlists

Decide which names deserve attention at all — technicals plus fundamentals, overnight, on the whole universe.

TIME

The Trigger Board

Decide which of those are live right now. It does not re-qualify; it watches.

TRIGGER

Section 4

Decide whether to act on this bar, and produce the plan — entry, stop, targets.

MANAGE

Risk Shield & Pyramid

Decide what happens after the fill — trail, add, trim, exit.

The doctrine that makes it work — inherited qualification

The watchlists QUALIFY; the board TIMES. A name arriving from a rigorous scan has already earned its place, so the board does not re-run the screen on it — it carries its source archetype and the board reports only its timing state. Fundamentals rank rather than block, and a “still valid” break-down guard replaces the re-screen.

Before this, only the broad catalyst scan produced actionable output and the most rigorous lists dead-ended at “no catalyst”. Re-qualifying what was already qualified throws away the work.

The two-stage rule — deliberate, do not collapse it

The board arms early and Section 4 fires strictly. That mismatch is intentional: the board's job is to narrow your attention to a handful of names, and S4's job is to say whether this bar is the one. The board arms, S4 executes. Making them agree would destroy the value of both.

The DNA rule

Zero signal drift. Change a value in the owner and it must reach every consumer in the same commit. Where a platform genuinely cannot match — TradingView's data-call limits, for instance — the gap is documented as intentional, never left to be discovered.

Why there are two bull scans, not one

Phase 4.6 screens the names the matcher already narrowed to. Phase 4.7 scans catalyst first, across the whole universe. Without it the funnel collapsed to a handful of names and catalysts appeared to vanish — the scan order itself was the bug.

Section 5The board — timing, not qualifying

The Trigger Board takes every qualified name and answers one question per row: is this live now? It runs one evaluator, so the board and the single-symbol view agree by construction rather than by luck.

ColumnWhat it actually means
ArchetypeWhich list qualified it — breakout, pullback, leader, recovery, catalyst-scan, at-value, or a pyramid add on something you already hold
CategoryPure timing state: invalidated · wait · armed · trigger live
S4-GOHow close it is to Section 4's execution gate, as n/4. A preview, not the verdict
OverallThe ranking. Sorted descending by default
LocWhich location sources place it — zone, S/R, AVWAP, value area
Two things the board does that are easy to misread

It refreshes on bar close, not continuously — once per 75m or 125m bar, plus a settle delay. A board built mid-bar shows patterns that can fade by the close, which is exactly the disagreement that used to look like a bug.

The Armed Register persists a plan. An alert firing days later still has its levels even after the watchlists have churned underneath it.

Section 6Section 4 — the execution layer

S4 owns the trigger and the final word. Its gate is four things, and the panel prints them as chips so you can see which one is missing:

GO  =  PA  ·  LOCATION  ·  VOLUME  ·  BAR

PA        a pattern from the battery fired on this bar
LOCATION  price is at a place worth buying — demand zone, S/R shelf, AVWAP, value area
VOLUME    relative volume clears the floor
BAR       the bar itself is not a distribution candle

Underneath the gate sits the part that took longest to get right: location. A leg-base-leg zone engine runs on the chart timeframe plus daily, weekly and monthly, with zones ageing per timeframe and moving fresh → tested → violated; alongside it, horizontal S/R levels that weaken as they are tested, three anchored VWAPs, and volume-profile levels.

The verdict is a ruling, not a readout

S4 does not just print values — it rules: take it · skip · not tradeable · low quality · arm and wait, naming the one gate that is missing and what to watch. A Stage 3/4 name is refused outright, before any other reasoning, because a plan reasoned inside the wrong frame is worse than no plan.

Read the plan off S4, not the board

S4 latches the trigger bar, so its entry, stop and targets are anchored to the bar that actually fired. The board shows levels at current price. When they differ, S4 is the plan of record.

Section 7Between “armed” and “ordered”

The gap between the system saying yes and the order going in is where the most expensive mistakes live. Two things sit in it.

The written review

One tool call reads the whole S4 panel; the reply comes back in five fixed blocks — what the system says, what you want to do, the divergence, the one question, and the sentence you write if you override. It never talks you into a trade: the chart-read has veto power, not entry power.

Full procedure on 24 · The Pre-Trade Gate.

Sizing and the pre-trade gate

Size is computed from risk budget and stop distance, then a hardened gate checks the order against the book before it can be placed: maximum open positions, the sector cap, per-trade risk as a share of equity, and a duplicate guard. Entries are gated; exits never are — blocking a sell is more dangerous than allowing a buy.

Section 8Managing the position

Every open position sits on a five-rung ladder, evaluated in priority order:

RungFires when
EXITAt the stop, deeply underwater, Stage 4, price structure broken, trail stopped out, or the time stop expired
TRIMWinners only — a target hit, over-extension, or earnings within days
REDUCESoft deterioration — lagging rotation, below the 50-day, weak score
ADDLeader and at location — both required, and capped on extension so it cannot chase
HOLDEverything else
Two things worth internalising about exits

Swing or positional is decided by a ladder, not a guess — declared timeframe, then the setup prefix, then the risk actually taken in ATR, then the chart. The entry-fixed rungs come first deliberately: a classifier that reads today's chart would rewrite the stop multiplier mid-trade.

The trail owns roughly 88% of positional exits, the initial stop about 12%. Targets are near-decorative — only a small minority of trades ever reach 3R. That makes the trail the least-tested lever in the system and the stop not the problem, despite four separate studies that went looking for one.

Section 9The record

A daily job reconciles the journal to the live broker book — adding holdings it is missing, correcting drifted quantities, and closing positions only when there is a completing sell with an authoritative price. It aborts entirely if the book comes back empty, because an API hiccup must never read as “everything was sold”.

Every new position captures a true entry snapshot — setup, stage, alpha, RS, conviction — so realised P&L can later be attributed to the signal that caused it rather than guessed at.

Section 12Standing rules and traps

A recompile destroys your alerts

Alerts bind to the compiled script. Recompiling S4 does not stale them — it deletes them. Recreating every GO alert after every compile is mandatory, not housekeeping.

Source bindings are positional and are dropped on recompile

S4 reads values from the dashboard by input source, which binds by position. Any recompile drops all of them, and an unbound source silently reads price rather than erroring. Re-run the binding script after every compile.

Pine has four ceilings, and only three announce themselves

Compiled tokens, main-body statements, plot outputs — and execution time, which only shows up as a timeout on a live chart. A library relieves the first; nothing relieves the last but doing less work per bar.

How to read any result

Split by catalyst family before concluding anything. Pooling has produced a false conclusion three separate times here. Measure stops and sizing in R, never per-trade percent — a percentage metric structurally rewards a wider stop and has already inverted one reported conclusion. And never judge a positional or recovery setup on a short window.

Section 13Thirty common mistakes

The most durable section in the original Bible. Nothing here has been superseded — the tools changed, these did not.

Discovery

  1. Skipping the sector read. Screening without knowing which sectors are Stage 3/4 fills the shortlist with names you must later discard.
  2. Setting the quality floor too low. It floods you with marginal setups.
  3. Disabling the recovery regime gate. In a strong bull market, “recovery” setups are usually ordinary pullbacks.
  4. Running on a stale broker token. The sync fails silently.
  5. Not keeping the shortlist. You lose the scores, stops and targets by the time you reach the chart.

Screening

  1. Confusing catalyst names across surfaces. Map them before comparing.
  2. Not checking live volume on a breakout. A breakout on below-average volume is a trap, and overnight data only sees the close.
  3. Entering a gap trade too early. Gap openings are often reversed in the first hour; the rule is above the first-candle high, after volume confirms.
  4. Taking the earliest recovery signal standalone. It is the highest-risk edge and needs context confirmation.
  5. Ignoring a short time limit on a mean-reversion trade. One that fails to revert quickly becomes a trend-following loser.

Validation

  1. Acting on a fundamentals-failed signal. Most should be skipped.
  2. Leaving override fields populated. They mask the real position data for that ticker.
  3. Not cross-checking context. A buy signal in cautionary context is not a valid entry.
  4. Trusting a buy in a Stage 3 sector. The sector reading is authoritative — never override it.
  5. Treating “improving” as “leading”. Improving means still underperforming, just less badly. A watch condition, not a full-conviction buy.

Context

  1. Running the context layer alongside the modules it replaces. Duplicate labels and conflicting drawings.
  2. Over-relying on a stale Wyckoff event. It persists until replaced and still scores. Check how many bars ago; older than about thirty is history, not a trigger.
  3. Misreading a liquidity sweep as a reversal. If you are long and the trend is intact, it is often continuation — the market took liquidity to fuel the next leg.
  4. Using a daily-length volume-profile lookback on weekly charts. The profile becomes too diffuse to mean anything.

Execution

  1. Chasing an extended breakout. Past a few percent above the level the reward has gone. Wait for the first pullback.
  2. Overriding the breakeven lock. It exists so a runner cannot become a loss.
  3. Adding before the first lot works. That is averaging up before the thesis is validated.
  4. Taking a breakout without the volatility squeeze. Without it you are entering a loose, high-risk break.

Recovery

  1. Using an outdated drawdown threshold. The gate is a band off the recent high with all pillars present, not a single deep number.
  2. Trading a recovery setup in bearish context. That stock is not recovering, it is still breaking down.
  3. Holding recovery trades through a macro reversal. The regime is the thesis — when it breaks, exit all of them.
  4. Placing the stop above the climax low. That low is the invalidation point for the whole thesis.

Portfolio

  1. Not recording entry dates. Without them the days-held counter and time stop cannot function.
  2. Exceeding the sector cap. When a sector runs there is a natural pull to load up. Rotations reverse violently and concentration makes the drawdown severe.
  3. Moving a stop locally and not at the broker. The next sync overwrites your change with the broker's value. Update at the broker first.
