Section 1System philosophy

Three schools, one edge

Stan Weinstein — stage analysis, the primary framework. Every stock is always in one of four stages. Stage 2 — under accumulation, trending up through its 30-week SMA — is the only stage where you want to own one. Stage 1 base-building, Stage 3 topping and Stage 4 decline are all avoided for long entries. The 30-week SMA is the watershed line, and stage transitions are the buy and sell triggers.

Mark Minervini — SEPA and the trend template, the entry filter. Within Stage 2, the six-MA trend template filters for the strongest names: 50 DMA > 150 DMA > 200 DMA, all rising, price above all three. VCP breakouts and EMA pullbacks provide the low-risk entry points. The Alpha Score (0–100) quantifies this numerically.

Wyckoff, SMC and Volume Profile — the context layer. Institutional footprints — volume shelf, order blocks, fair-value gaps, liquidity sweeps, POC/VAH/VAL — confirm that smart money is genuinely accumulating rather than distributing. Context Layers consolidates all three into a single CONTEXT SCORE from −12 to +12.

The core discipline

Never trade against macro. If CNX500 is in bear or the sector is Stage 3/4, no long setups exist — however good the individual stock looks. Macro kills more trades than any technical error.

Never skip the Dashboard. Python finds candidates. Pine filters on raw data. The Dashboard is the final judge. No position opens without ≥ BUY and Alpha ≥ 60.

Always cross-check the Context Score. A BUY from the Unified Ecosystem with a context score below BULL is a reduced-conviction entry. Size accordingly.

Never size into a signal you cannot defend. Know the stop before entry.

Critical — what never goes in a portfolio slot

Slots 1–30 are exclusively for open positions held in Dhan. They are not for watchlist candidates. Every slot metric — P&L, R-multiple, days held, time-stop warnings, trailed-stop alerts — only makes sense once a position is live with a real entry, stop and date. Put a watchlist ticker in a slot and all of them will lie to you.

Section 4The context layer — load first, change never

Before running any screener or reading any signal, load the context tools on a daily chart. They generate no entry signals. They tell you where you are in market structure and whether institutional activity supports the trade.

Zigzag [Strict] — the 11-row panel

RowWhat it tells you
TRENDUptrend / downtrend / sideways — developing-aware, flips live when a BoS or CHoCH is projected
MTF TRENDHigher-timeframe state. Confluence = full conviction; conflict = counter-trend
STRUCTUREHH / HL uptrend · LH / LL downtrend · EH / EL equal, sideways

CHOPPINESSFlips per 26W / 52W. ≤ 4 good · 5–8 caution · > 8 avoid
INVALIDATIONLatest swing low — your structural stop
BOS LEVELLatest swing high — your bullish trigger. Stays visible after the break as a retest reference

BAR AGEBars since the active pivot — swing maturity

Read together: an uptrend with MTF confluence, fresh bar age, healthy swing range, low choppiness, and a BoS level just broken on 2× volume is the textbook setup.

Context Layers — base, bonus, final

BASE SCORE  = Wyckoff (−4..+4) + VP (−3..+3) + SMC (−3..+3) + OB proximity (−2..+2)
SETUP BONUS = +2 Spring/LPS · +2 Sweep+CHoCH · +1 OB retest · −1 choppy · −2 distribution
FINAL SCORE = BASE + BONUS      range −14 to +14

Why a bonus at all? Without it, a confirmed Spring firing at a borderline base of 6 could not distinguish itself from a flat 6. The bonus tips it into STRONG BULL, making conviction visible on the panel. Anti-circularity is enforced by code order — the setup detector runs on the base score only, so a setup never sees its own bonus.

FinalBandMeaning
≥ 9STRONG BULLAll three modules aligned plus an active setup. Full size, 1.25× Kelly
4 to 8BULLMajority aligned. Normal size
−3 to +3NEUTRALMixed. Be selective, reduce 25–50%
−4 to −6CAUTIONMajority bearish. No new longs
≤ −7BEARAll aligned bearish. Flat or exit

The rule: enter only when FINAL SCORE is BULL or STRONG BULL and SETUP BONUS ≥ 0. A negative bonus comes from distribution or chop — both are sit-out signals even while the final score is still in the BULL band, because that score is stale.

Reading the modules

Wyckoff. SOS +4 is the strongest bullish event and an entry trigger. Spring and LPS +3 each are excellent entry zones. SC +2 is an early floor, not yet actionable. On the bear side SOW and LPSY are −4 each — exit immediately; UT −3, do not chase; BC −2, tighten stops. Always check the bars ago counter — an SOS from 5 bars ago is actionable, one from 80 bars ago is history that still scores.

Volume Profile. Above VAH +3, in the control zone with institutional backing. In the upper value area +1, healthy. Lower value area −1, weak. Below VAL −3, the rejection zone — no positional longs. Distance to POC between 0% and +5% is the ideal pullback entry.

SMC. A bull OB below price is demand; price inside it is maximum confluence. A clear bear OB overhead is the ideal entry environment. A liquidity sweep within 10 bars, combined with a bull OB, is one of the most reliable patterns in the module.

Section 5Phase 1 — pre-market intelligence

08:30–09:00 IST, in Commander Web. Assess macro risk, track institutional flows, synthesise the day's strategy.

  1. Global markets — overnight US closes, morning Asia.
  2. FII/DII — institutional net flows from the previous session.
  3. India VIX — a spiking VIX demands smaller sizing today.
  4. Breadth — the McClellan oscillator. Negative favours tightening stops over fresh longs.

Section 6Phase 2 — discovery and screening

Evening after close, or 30–45 minutes before open. Goal: 500 NSE stocks down to 15–25 candidates.

Sector first, always

Before touching any individual stock you must know the sector landscape. Stage 2 + LEADING sectors are the hunting grounds. Stage 1 is acceptable for early entries only. Stage 3 or 4 is a hard block regardless of the individual signal. LAGGING quadrant demands Alpha ≥ 80 even in Stage 2.

Section 7DThe confluence sequence

The nine-step sequence is the operational heart of this Bible — macro, sector, watchlist, screener, dashboard, context, structure, entry, sizing, each step's output gating the next. It is one-directional: if a step fails at its hard floor the candidate is dead for that day, and you do not make up for it with a strong reading downstream.

Section 11Trade management

Adding to winners

Add a second lot only when all five hold:

  1. The first lot is ≥ 1R in profit and the breakeven lock is active
  2. Alpha Score has not declined since entry
  3. Vol shelf is still confirmed
  4. Context score is still BULL or STRONG BULL
  5. A genuinely new lower-risk entry exists — an EMA20 pullback or a fresh mini-VCP break

Pyramid lot is 50% of the initial lot. Never exceed 10% of capital in one name.

Trailing stops

Chandelier trail, positional. After T1 the breakeven lock is active and the trail keeps rising with price. The step-line is plotted on the chart — never manually override it downward. Stage 4 is an immediate full close; do not wait for the trail.

EMA20 ratchet, swing. The stop rises each bar to the greater of the current stop and the EMA20 less the buffer. Kill switch: a close below the SMA50 exits immediately regardless of where the trail sits.

Gap-downs against the position

Below the stopExit at market on open. Do not wait for a recovery
Above but close to itCheck Context Layers. If Wyckoff reads ACCUMULATION and a new SC or Spring is forming, hold with the stop intact. Otherwise exit before it violates
AlwaysNever average down on a loss. The initial stop is sacrosanct

The time-stop protocol

  1. Is there a structural reason for the stagnation — sector underperformance, earnings blackout, broad weakness? If the thesis is intact and the cause is macro, one extension of 5 days is permitted.
  2. No macro reason, or the extension also produced nothing → exit.
  3. Log it as time-stopped, and review whether the setup was ambiguous or the catalyst weak.

SWG-REV is the exception — its time stop is 5 days, not 10. If mean reversion has not happened in 5 days the setup has failed. Exit regardless of everything else.

Section 12Phase 5 — post-market

After entry

  1. Note the active catalyst label on the chart — this locks in the trail type for the trade.
  2. Set three alerts: T1 (partial exit), initial stop (exit reminder), and 90% of the time-stop date.
  3. Run Portfolio Sync to inject the position into the Dashboard.

After exit

Clear or overwrite the portfolio slot, then re-run Portfolio Sync.

Weekly review, Sunday evening

  1. Portfolio Sync to snapshot week-end positions
  2. Sector Dashboard — note stage transitions
  3. Bull Screener — are current holdings still top-quartile by Alpha?
  4. Recovery Screener — has any recovery position reached Stage 2 and earned promotion to the bull track?
  5. Update stops the Chandelier moved during the week
  6. If the recent win rate dropped below 40%, cut to 0.75× Kelly until it recovers
  7. Load Context Layers on every holding — anything at CAUTION gets its trail tightened by half

Section 15Thirty common mistakes, by stage

The most durable section in this document — none of these has been superseded.

At the Python discovery stage

  1. Skipping the Sector Dashboard. Screening without first checking which sectors are Stage 3/4 fills your shortlist with names you will be forced to discard later. Sector first, always.
  2. Setting min Alpha too low. At 40–50 the screener floods you with marginal setups. Floor is 60; raise to 70 in extended bull markets.
  3. Disabling the regime gate on Recovery. Not optional. In strong bull markets, “recovery” setups are usually just normal pullbacks.
  4. Running scans on a stale Dhan token. Portfolio Sync fails silently.
  5. Not exporting the shortlist. Without the CSV you lose scores, stops and targets by the time you reach Pine.

At the Pine re-screening stage

  1. Confusing the catalyst names. POS-AC was POS-ACCUM; GAP-GO was SWG-GAP. Map them before the Pine step.
  2. Not checking live volume on a POS-BO. A breakout on below-average volume is a trap, and Python only sees end-of-day volume.
  3. Entering GAP-GO before 11:30. Gap openings are often reversed in the first hour. The rule is above the first-candle high, after volume confirms.
  4. Taking REV-EARLY standalone. The highest-risk recovery edge. It needs Context Layers to confirm accumulation and a context score ≥ neutral.
  5. Ignoring the SWG-REV 5-day limit. Mean-reversion trades that fail to revert quickly become trend-following losers.

At the Dashboard stage

  1. Acting on BUY* without checking fundamentals. The asterisk means the technical fired but both RFF conditions failed. Most BUY* signals should be skipped.
  2. Leaving Quick Check values populated. They override the portfolio slot for that ticker. Reset both to zero after use.
  3. Not cross-checking the context score. A Dashboard BUY at CAUTION is not a valid entry. At NEUTRAL it gets 50% size at most.
  4. Trusting a BUY in a Stage 3 sector. The SECTOR row is authoritative — never override it.
  5. Treating IMPROVING as equivalent to LEADING. Improving means the stock is still underperforming, just less badly. It is a watch condition, not a full-conviction buy.

At the Context Layers stage

  1. Running Context Layers alongside the three original modules. It replaces them. Running all four creates duplicate labels, doubled FVG boxes and conflicting VP lines.
  2. Over-relying on a stale Wyckoff event. LAST EVENT persists until replaced — an SOS from 60 bars ago still scores +4. Events older than 30 bars are context, not triggers.
  3. Misreading a downward liquidity sweep as a short trigger. If you are long and SMC trend is still bullish, it is often continuation — the market took liquidity to fuel the next leg. React only if a bearish CHoCH follows.
  4. Using a 100-bar VP lookback on weekly charts. That is nearly two years; the profile becomes too diffuse. Use 52 on weekly.

At the execution stage

  1. Chasing a POS-BO more than 3% extended. Past that the risk/reward has deteriorated. Wait for the first pullback to the breakout level.
  2. Not moving to breakeven after T1. Handled automatically — do not override or delete the breakeven floor.
  3. Pyramiding before 1R. Adding before the first lot works is averaging up before the thesis is validated.
  4. Using SWG-BO without VCP tight. Without the squeeze you are entering a loose, high-risk breakout.

At the recovery stage

  1. Applying the old 25% drawdown threshold. The gate is drawdown from the 60-bar high with all four pillars, not 25% from the 52-week high.
  2. Trading REV-CB in BEAR context. A stock in full bear context is not recovering, it is breaking down further.
  3. Holding recovery trades through a macro reversal. If CNX500 falls through the regime gate, exit all recovery positions. No exceptions.
  4. Setting the stop above the climax bar's low. That low is the invalidation point for the entire recovery thesis.

At the portfolio stage

  1. Not syncing entry dates. Without the date, the days-held counter and time-stop warning cannot function.
  2. Exceeding the 25% sector cap. When a sector runs there is a natural pull to load up on it. Sector rotations reverse violently and concentration makes the drawdown severe.
  3. Forgetting to re-sync after moving a stop. Portfolio Sync will overwrite your manual value with the last broker-fetched one. Update at the broker first.
