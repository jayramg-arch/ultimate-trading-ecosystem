Process · GM + S4 ecosystem

The Operating Loop

The machine qualifies overnight, you arm in the evening, the alerts watch the session, and you only open a chart when one fires. Everything here is either automatic or one of two moments that need judgement — and the second of those is reading the S4 panel, which now has its own section.

Foundation

Four stages — and what never to ask each one

Every surface answers exactly one question. Most of the confusion this system has produced came from asking a surface something it was never built to answer.

The two-stage doctrine inside that

The GM fires early — a name is armed. S4 is the strict execution gate — a trade is triggered. They are deliberately not aligned. GM arms → you focus → you wait for the S4 GO. Collapsing them would destroy the thing that makes the shortlist useful.

GTT Trailautomatic

Tighten-only Chandelier on the live OCO stop legs, catalyst-aware (POS 4.5× / SWG 1.5× ATR). Telegrams a summary. Never loosens a stop, never sells.

Live again since 9 September as a Windows Task Scheduler job (GTT_Trail_Daily, 15:45 Mon–Fri, run_gtt_trail.bat) rather than a job inside the app's scheduler, which had fired exactly once on 24 July and never again while three stops sat 7–17% under their Chandelier. Pre-flight lints the code first and Telegrams any fault, so a broken job shows up as a message instead of silence. Every run logs to logs/gtt_shield.log.

Two OCOs per name is the normal case (11 of 15 holdings), and both stop legs are trailed; a stop that has already run past its Chandelier is reported BREACHED and never sold by the machine.

Disabled since 3 Oct (Dhan refuses every modify without a whitelisted IP), so you trail by hand off v67's Chandelier line — which, from v67.4.26 (4 Oct), is Risk Shield's number: the auto-pilot's Phase 12f pushes each holding's window, multiplier and floor into v67. After you edit a holding's Timeframe, setup or manual override in the journal, press 🔄 Sync to TV on the Journal page. The same push also fills v67's portfolio slots, so the old copy-the-code-and-compile step is gone; the auto-pilot repeats it every evening.

Stops you move on Dhan reach v67 by themselves (4 Oct). The 16:30 journal sync reads your resting Dhan orders and writes each holding's stop and targets into the journal — the same rule the Journal page applies when it opens — and the auto-pilot's Phase 12f pushes them into v67's slots. The stop taken is your most recently placed or modified stop order. Same day, without waiting: open the Journal page (it reads the new stops), then press Sync to TV.

Daily check on Risk Shield (5 Oct). Two warnings near the top are the list to fix on Dhan: Partly covered (shares with no stop) and Stops disagree (one holding, two stop prices — every live stop fires, the higher one first). The Journal page and the 16:30 log carry the same warnings. Three rules for clean stops:

  • One stop price per holding. Dhan re-stamps an order's time on any edit, even a quantity change, so with two prices live the stop the journal and v67 show flips with whichever order you touched last (VIJAYA, 5 Oct).
  • Stops cover every share. The first check found 394 of 526 COALINDIA, 67 of 98 VIJAYA and 29 of 58 LAURUSLABS shares with no stop; all fixed the same night.
  • Limit below the trigger (e.g. trigger 1268.5, limit 1267.5). A sell stop-limit with its limit at the trigger can go unfilled when price falls straight through it.

Review and arm← you · ~15 min

The one step that needs judgement. Everything else today is a machine or a reaction to one.

  1. Restart clean. STOP_COMMANDER.bat → relaunch. A browser reload does not reload code.
  2. Open the Golden Matcher window (the sidebar entry opens it in its own window since 13 Sep). Phase 12 has already built all three boards and the bundles; the build stamp says when. Press 🌙 Evening run only if the stamp is old, the auto-pilot failed, or you recompiled — one click does the fetch, the three boards and the option chains.
  3. Read the header before the rows. Failure counts, source-issue strip, provenance, AGE. An empty source list means part of the shortlist is silently missing.
  4. Work top-down. The board opens filtered to all-gates, sorted by Overall. Open Single Symbol on anything worth attention. Its S4-GO column counts the same five gates S4 chips; a row tagged F? passed the fifth by default because the books were never scored. The board predicts; S4 decides.

  6. The bundles reach S4 by themselves. Since 21 Sep the Evening run pushes both bundles into every S4 tab over the debug port (PUSH_BUNDLES is the by-hand button, --check shows what each tab holds). No pasting. Then read S4's header: S4 v11.4 │ core/53 │ 10511ch·15/15 — the receipt (since 20 Sep) says the bundle arrived whole; ⚠tail? means a short paste, ⚠b2 a second field without OPT. Everything else is folded away in a hand-overrides expander.

About a minute a name, ninety seconds for an ETF (its index is read first). Rulings land in logs/ai_reviews/ and on the Reviewer Log page (Doc 31). Take the R numbers from the canon line it prints, not from the prose. Doc 32 is the manual.

Auto-pilot Phase 13 queues every 5/5 GO on the Daily, 125m and 75m boards on the AI reviewer (about 90 s each, one at a time), labelled board in the Reviewer Log. on ping

Execute← you

The second moment that needs judgement — and it is only as good as the arming was, because an armed name is one you have already thought about.

Every evening

The alert ritual

The auto-pilot writes a new, date-stamped TradingView watchlist each evening, so yesterday's alert points at yesterday's list by construction.

  1. Check the source-issue strip first. On a thin auto-pilot day you would otherwise arm a near-empty watchlist and lose a day of coverage silently.
  2. Delete yesterday's alert. Left running it fires on names that already dropped out, and eats the alert quota.
  3. Create today's — condition S4 GO (PA + Location + Volume + Bar + Funda), trigger Once Per Bar Close, app push on, Webhook URL https://<your ngrok domain>/s4-review?key=… (from .env), named to match the watchlist: S4 GO · GM-16SEP · 75m. Do not edit yesterday's instead — it points at a list that no longer exists and fails to save.
  4. Repeat on a 125m chart. An alert inherits the chart's resolution, so each timeframe needs its own.
TimeframeBar closes
75m10:30 · 11:45 · 13:00 · 14:15 · 15:30
125m11:20 · 13:25 · 15:30

125m is not just "slower". Its first close is 11:20 — after the opening drive, whereas 75m's first close is the opening drive. It skips the noisiest bar of the session by construction, at the cost of two fewer ping windows.

Two pings, one setup

A name firing on both timeframes is one setup seen twice, not corroboration — same engine, same gates, only the sampling rate differs. They share the 15:30 close, so duplicates cluster there. Check the panel on the timeframe you intend to act on and treat the other as an echo.

Two things worth knowing

Recreating daily also refreshes the GM lists frozen into the alert at creation, so pullback context never goes stale. And every S4 recompile deletes all alerts — they bind to the compiled script id — so recreating after a compile is mandatory, not housekeeping.

The execution path

Read the SUMMARY before the rows. It is the only field on the panel that weighs one section against another, and its last line tells you which case you are in. It is also a fixed template — it can restate rows, it cannot argue — which is what the reviewer is for when the ruling and your read disagree:

SL is where you get out. INVAL is where the reason you bought stops being true. If they are the same price, you have no room to be wrong — wait for a deeper entry.

At the chart

Reading the S4 panel

Forty-odd rows in six sections. They are not equal, and they are not read top to bottom — the panel reasons in an order, and following it is what stops you arguing yourself into a trade one row at a time. Section Five sits on the second chart: the real footprint delta, the Wyckoff ladder and the geometry read live there, because they cannot share a chart with S4 without killing its panel.

ReadSectionThe questionIf it fails
1stSUMMARY (right column)What does the whole panel mean together?start here always
2ndI · Macro & contextDoes this name belong on a chart at all?close the chart
3rdII · Location, value & roomIs this price the place? (Room is information here — measured 11 Sep, it does not predict outcome.)wait for a pullback
4thIII · Execution & timingIs it firing right now?let the alert wait
5thIV · Decision synthesisThe ruling, and the one blocking gate—
6thV · Plan & riskWhat do I type into the broker?—

The order matters more than any single row. Section II can be perfect on a name Section I has already disqualified — and a good location is exactly what makes a bad name tempting. Reading downward means the disqualifier arrives before the temptation.

The five gates, and the two that do not fire

The TRIGGER row shows six chips. Five of them can stop a trade:

ChipGateBlank means
PA pattern fired on the last closed barnothing has triggered — the alert has not gone off yet
LPrice is at a location the engine can nameno zone, level, AVWAP or S/R under price — nothing defines wrong
VRelative volume clears the floorthe move has no participation behind it
BThe trigger bar closed stronga red or weak-closing bar — the pattern fired into selling

RRRG rotation — chipped but off by defaultit never vetoes; it is there to be seen, not obeyed
Why F exists at all

It is the first non-technical term in the gate, and it is there because "I do not want the trade outcome to be entirely based on technicals." S4 cannot compute it — TradingView caps financial calls, and BFF needs a growth table no Pine surface can reach — so it does not judge, it applies the score the board already computed and you pasted in. A blank F on a name you know is sound usually means you have not pasted the bundle, not that the company is weak.

The five drawn levels

One toggle puts up to five dashed rays on the chart: the futures long and short basis, option max pain, and the strikes with the most written PE and CE open interest. Dashed and labelled so they never read as your own zones. Fewer than five is normal — a futures side needs three OI prints before it draws at all.

These grade. They never gate.

None of this is validated. Round numbers were measured and did not replicate; Wyckoff was rejected twice, as a veto and as a score. Options positioning is a monthly-expiry structure and your book runs 8–12 weeks to 6–8 months, so it informs the next few weeks — entry and first target — and says nothing about the thesis. Treat it exactly like arrival and delta: it can talk you out of a trade, never into one.

After ~20 F&O rows, entered-below-the-writers versus entered-above can be compared against realised R, and this becomes evidence or it goes in the bin with round numbers.

Swing or positional (7 Oct). Classify at entry from the list the GO came from (GM_Positional = positional, GM_Swing = swing; S4's Plan row breaks a tie) and write it into the journal Timeframe. A swing becomes positional only through the Risk Shield conversion check, judged on a Daily close: working (at T1, or the new stop above entry), the rule reads positional, Stage 2 weekly, sector not Stage 4. The new stop is never closer than 4 × ATR(D) and the quantity is trimmed to the house risk. A losing swing converts only at half risk (salvage); otherwise its swing stop applies. Every weekly close, positional holdings that read Stage 3/4 go to the exit/reduce review — the evening digest lists them under TRADE CLASS.

88% of positional exits come from the trail, and only 8.4% ever reach 3R. Targets are upside; the trail is the mechanism. Do not tighten it — five stop studies have rejected changing it, the fifth (9 Sep) on the trigger basis rather than the distance: exiting on the daily close instead of the intraday low rescued the wicks and paid for it exactly in worse fills.
