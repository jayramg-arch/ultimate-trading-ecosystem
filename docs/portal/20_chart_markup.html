01You are running v2.11, and the guide stops at v2.2

02The pivot — nine revisions of tuning, answered by a drag handle

The admission in the v2.9 header, and it is the most useful line in the file

“The premise of v1.0 to v2.8 was that a scoring function could be tuned until it drew the line your eye wants. It cannot: optimal is chart-specific, so every field test moved the weights and broke the previous fix.”

Look at the sequence and the pattern is unmistakable — recency bias, then confluence bias, then angle bias, then strict touch dominance, then per-side tolerances, then an asymmetric touch depth. Six attempts at a weighting that generalises, each prompted by one chart that came out wrong, each breaking the chart the last one fixed.

v2.9 stopped. Auto-detection became a seed; you drag the line to where it belongs; and — the part that matters — the script reads your handle back. Your level flows into the panel, the plan, the alerts. It is not an annotation layer on top of the engine, it is an input to it.

BehaviourDetail
Untouched is unchangedA handle left at zero means “use auto”, so a chart you have not marked behaves exactly as before
Manual outranks autoYour marked level wins the panel and the plan even when an average or a retracement sits a few ticks nearer
Manual replaces, never doublesA hand-placed trendline replaces the auto fit for that side rather than drawing alongside it
Provenance is visibleYour levels draw in their own colour and tag ·man in the panel, so you always know which the plan actually used
Auto-namedA dragged level is named for whatever structure it lands nearest — 52-week extreme, an average, the anchored curve — else just “manual”. The script cannot know what you meant; it reports what you hit
Why v2.10 was needed a day later

v2.9 merged manual and auto levels into one flat nearest-wins pool, so a level you had deliberately marked could lose to an average a few ticks closer. It drew on the chart and never reached the panel or the plan — which inverts the doctrine. Your hand-drawn levels ARE the location layer; the auto ones are the fallback for a side you have not marked yet.

Same release fixed a subtler one: every delete lived inside its own show-toggle, so turning a feature off skipped its cleanup and froze that drawing on the chart permanently. Tuning had been happening against stale levels from earlier settings.

03The four stages

  1. Detect — swing pivots, breakaway gaps on volume, rolling 52-week extremes.
  2. Classify — Weinstein stage off the weekly 30-week anchor, and Mansfield strength against the broad index.
  3. Annotate — trendlines, geometry, level clusters, classical patterns, the anchored curve, retracements.
  4. Verdict — a panel giving setup, conviction, nearest levels, and a computed plan.

Strength and stage reuse the same maths as the strength pane and the unified strategy; the anchored curve is ported from the dashboard and the retracements from the swing engine — zero drift by intent, with the other tools untouched.

Pivot lengths resolve from the chart's own timeframe — monthly 1, weekly 5, daily 2, intraday 2 — the same four numbers the structure engine uses. Heavy pivot-pair fitting runs only on the last bar.

04The touch rule, and the one hard invalidation

The correction that set the rule

Rejecting a line because a wick pierced it contradicted the method. A trendline may cut through wicks — through bodies, in the extreme. The only hard invalidation is a candle CLOSING beyond it. Wick rejection survives as an off-by-default strict mode.

v2.7 sharpened that into an asymmetric test: the line may cut deep into a wick and still count that pivot as a touch, while the other side stays tight. That asymmetry is the whole point — it is why a near-collinear set of four wicks now scores four touches instead of two, and wins, because touches dominate.

RuleSourceWhat it does
Primary versus return lineMageeIn a downtrend the trendline runs along the highs; in an uptrend along the lows. The label says which you are looking at
Distinct-touch spacingBulkowskiClustered touches a few bars apart count as ONE test. Otherwise a single consolidation inflates a line to five touches
Break filterMurphyA break alert needs a close a full volatility unit beyond the line, not a poke past the tolerance — the classic penetration rule, scaled to the instrument
Throwback memoryMagee · MurphyA just-broken line is frozen rather than discarded — broken support is the next resistance
Why the two sides were decoupled

A field test proved one shared fitting window could not satisfy both: tuning support wrecked resistance and vice versa. Each side now has its own window and its own tolerance, so the support line can loosen just enough to catch a near-collinear set of wicks without loosening resistance at all.

There is a debug toggle that marks every tracked pivot. Use it before blaming the fit — check the engine saw the swings your eye is using, because most “wrong line” complaints are really a pivot-detection disagreement.

05Timeframe-scoped levels — the weekend marking workflow

v2.11 replaced flat level slots with three groups: monthly, weekly, daily. The group is the timeframe, and that is not filing — it is the conviction.

RuleBehaviour
Higher timeframe down, never lower upA level renders on its own timeframe and every lower one. Monthly shows everywhere; a daily level never appears on a weekly chart. Same guardrail the zone engine uses — not a second invented rule
The timeframe rides to the panelSupport and resistance rows carry ·M, ·W, ·D
Line weight encodes it tooMonthly heaviest, daily lightest
Role is derived, not filedSupport or resistance comes from price versus close, so a level flips as price closes through it rather than going stale as a mislabelled resistance underfoot
Ties go to the higher timeframeA monthly and a daily level at the same price reads monthly. Conviction takes the higher reading, never the lower
Why this is a sizing feature, not a display one

A stop on a weekly level is a deeper distal, which means a wider stop, which means size down. Reading “1,718.00 · −2.1%” without knowing whether that floor is a daily shelf or a monthly one is how a position gets mis-sized.

Marking deliberately stops at daily. The lower timeframes are a timing lens, not a zone surface — that is the zone engine's job, and duplicating it here would create a second set of levels to keep in sync.

06Geometry

Trend, not parallelNeither parallel nor convergingDirectional but untidy

A falling wedge inside a decline is the one worth flagging: the down-move is running out of room, which is what a base completing looks like before the stage engine will say so.

The apex prints its price and how many bars ahead it sits.

07The panel, row by row

Four sections — state, levels, plan, verdict. It is built to be read top to bottom as a trade card.

RowWhat it carries
StageThe digit, a mark, and bars in stage — freshness matters as much as the number
Relative strengthValue, a trend arrow, and leads or lags
SetupWhich play this is — section 08
TrendThe geometry, plus the apex and its distance when converging
ConvictionScore out of seven — section 09
Resistance · SupportNearest level each side with the distance, tagged by timeframe and by whether it is yours
Anchored curveIts value, and whether price is above it
PatternsWhat was detected, with a confirmation mark or a candidate tag
Entry · Stop · TargetThe computed plan with risk percentage and reward-to-risk — a dash on any non-bullish setup

VerdictThe one-line action

The plan's stop is capped at a volatility multiple and tags itself when the cap binds — so a nearest-support that happens to sit a long way down cannot quietly produce a stop no sizing rule would accept.

08Setup and verdict

ConditionSetupVerdict
Stage 4 with negative strengthDeclineExit or avoid — no new longs
The 30-week average reclaimed recently, stage 1 or 2, strength positiveReversalEmerging long — confirm strength is actually rising
Stage 2, averages stacked, strength positive, near the 50-dayPullbackBuy the dip — entry on the reclaim, stop below the average
Same, but near the 52-week highAt highsBuy strength — and watch the extension
Same, mid-rangeUptrendHold, add on a pullback to the average
Stage 1BaseWatch. Needs a breakout and positive strength
Anything elseNeutralNo edge — wait

The pullback row is the highest-quality of these — a leader resting on a rising average, with a tight stop just underneath. The at-highs row is the one to be careful with: it is a real setup and it is also where extension risk lives, which is why the verdict names both halves.

09Conviction

Seven independent checks, one point each: stage 2 · strength positive · strength rising · above the 50-day · above the anchored curve · above the 200-day · volume rising.

ScoreGradeSuggested sizing
6–7STRONGFull position at standard risk — everything aligns
4–5MODERATEHalf or a probe. Add when the missing factor confirms, not before
≤ 3WEAKNo trade. The structure is not there yet

It answers one question well — how many forces agree — and the value is that a headline can look bullish while four of the seven quietly disagree.

The averages behind it are anchored to daily on every chart timeframe, so the score does not change meaning when you drop to an intraday view.

10Where it sits

This is the breadth tool. It answers what is this and is it worth my time on any chart in seconds. It does not replace the deep read.

LayerToolWhen
BreadthThis, read off the panelSweeping a watchlist or the screener
DepthYour own marked levels and trendlineThe handful you will actually size

By timeframe

WeeklyThe stage truth. Confirm a daily setup is a real stage change and not a bounce. Raise the pivot strength
DailyThe primary canvas — the defaults are tuned here
IntradayEntry timing inside a confirmed daily setup. Patterns are faster and noisier

The reading order is the gate: stage and strength first, then conviction, then the setup, then the levels, then the plan. Take the trade only where reward-to-risk clears 2 and the plan is not a dash.

11Alerts

AlertFires on
Trendline break, either sideA close through the line — past the volatility filter, not just the tolerance
Level cluster break, either sideA close through the nearest cluster
Pattern neckline confirmsA double or triple formation, or a head and shoulders
Pullback triggerA stage-2 pullback reclaiming the daily 50-day
Conviction turns strongThe score crossing to six
Stage changeThe weekly classification flipping

All are edge-triggered — once per state change, not once per bar. The panel's break row shows the exact values the trendline alerts are watching, so you can see what an alert would need before it fires.

12Honest limits

LimitDetail
Inputs do not follow the symbolSwitch symbols and your dragged levels stay put — and will be wrong. Reset to zero or keep a per-symbol layout. There is no way around this in Pine, and it is why a heavily-marked name gets its own file
Auto lines are a seedThey get you most of the way. The line you trade should be one you placed — which is better discipline anyway, and since v2.9 the script reads it
Candidate patterns are heuristicHead-and-shoulders and cup formations fire on noise. Confirm by eye; never trade the tag
The plan is mechanicalEntry at the close, stop near support, target near resistance. A starting plan, to be refined against your own structure and volatility sizing
It needs a clean feedStrength and stage will mis-read on a delayed or illiquid one
It finds; you decideThe job is to surface and frame candidates fast. The decision, the size and the execution stay yours

In one line: scan with the panel, gate on stage plus strength plus conviction, trade only the strong ones at reward-to-risk of two or better — and mark the levels yourself, because since v2.9 the engine will use them.
