01Why it exists

S4 sits at its compiled-token ceiling, and every S4 compile costs an S4Core publish, a re-bind on every tab and fresh alert versions. A small display read is not worth that. Since 12 October each one goes into this indicator as a module, so it costs a single Add-ons compile and nothing else.

ModuleSinceWhat it showsFeeds anything?
M1 · Latest bar read12 OctWeak / neutral / strong on the last closed bar, plus the bars before itNo. The board's Bar column uses the same four numbers
M2 · v2 status18 Octv2's group, rank against the cut, holding, and its exit levels, on any v2 nameNo. Read from a string pushed nightly
M3 · Candlesticks27 Oct22 candlestick patterns, drawn only at a key level, with confirmation marksDisplay only. Since v1.5 S4 prints the newest one (row 22 and SUMMARY), so the reviewer reads it. Never a grade
Chart patterns live elsewhere

Double and triple bottoms, head and shoulders, cup with handle and the VCP are in S5 (Doc 30, §04b), because they are built from S5's pivot and geometry engines. Add-ons M3 is candlesticks only: one to three bars, read at a level.

02Rules for a module

  1. Display only. A module never feeds S4's GO or an alert. It may export a display-only read that S4 binds and prints (M3, since v1.5); there too it never grades or gates. If a read earns a place in the gate, it moves into S4 or S4Core at a compile that is happening anyway.
  2. Its own input group with an Enable switch, so any module can be turned off alone.
  3. Its own section in the file, self-contained, with no shared state between modules.
  4. Numbers shared with Python are constants, not inputs, and a test checks the pair (M1: tests/test_bar_read.py; M3: tests/test_addons_m3.py).
  5. It shares Pine's limits like any other script: 64 plots, compiled tokens, 40 request calls, 20 s. Prefer labels and shapes; avoid request calls unless needed.

03M1 · Latest bar read

Weak.C:36%.TW:64% | 1b:W 2b:N 3b:S

S4's B gate passes a bar that closed green or in the upper half of its range, so a green bar with a long top wick passes. That is loose on purpose: the GO is an alert trigger, and tightening it was measured three times in August and cost winners. You read the latest closed bar by eye before ordering; M1 names that read.

GradeRuleMark
WeakClose in the lower third of the range, or an upper wick of half the range or moreOrange dot above the bar (on by default)
StrongClose in the upper third and an upper wick under a quarter of the rangeTeal triangle below (off by default)
NeutralEverything else—

The label sits on the last closed bar: C is the close's position in the range, TW the top wick's share of it. After the bar the label lists the previous bars' grades (1b:W = one bar back was weak), because a price-action trigger may have fired up to three bars back on a bearish bar while the latest bar is bullish. Order on the latest bar; the PA bar is context.

On Daily, TradingView keeps the day's bar unconfirmed after 15:30; M1 treats it as closed from then, the same rule S4 uses.

A read, not a rule

The four thresholds equal the board's Bar column (gm_trigger_board.BAR_*). Whether the grade separates outcomes is pre-registered as H2 in docs/PREREG_rejection_at_level.md and will be judged on rows from 12 October onward.

04M2 · v2 system status

v2 · HOLD · rank 14/120 · cut 30 · your holding
exit if the decision close < 100-DMA 1,842.30
decided 2026-10-19

The v2 system decides at Tuesday's close in Python. On the chart of any v2 name M2 prints that decision: the group (BUY · SELL · HOLD · WATCH), the rank against the buy cut, whether the holding is real or paper, and the decision date. The text is a single line pushed every evening by tv_push_addons.py (auto-pilot, and step 5 of AFTER_COMPILE) from data/v2/addon_status.txt. Do not edit the input; the next push overwrites it.

v2 has no stop. It leaves on Clenow's exits, and M2 draws them, but only on a name v2 owns or is buying this week (on a WATCH name, often a v1 holding, they would read as an instruction they are not):

  • The 100-day average of daily closes (orange line). A decision close below it is sold at the next open; the label says BELOW NOW when it is.
  • The previous daily close ±15% (dotted). A one-day move beyond either makes the name ineligible for 90 days.
  • Flags from Python: X not in the Nifty 500 (sold at the next rebalance), S NSE surveillance (out of the pool), 1 held by v1 (a v2 buy is a CONVERT; see the order sheet).

S4's VERDICT and GO rows are the current system's entry rules and do not apply to v2 names. M2 is the v2 read. The 100-DMA and the gap band are computed on the chart's own daily bars, so they cannot drift from what you see.

05M3 · Candlesticks at key levels (v1.4; S4 export v1.5, 27 Oct)

Hmr ? ↺ BuE ✓ MS TwB ✗

Twenty-two candlestick patterns, eleven bullish and eleven bearish, each with its own switch. They are drawn only where they can mean something: at one of S4's own S/R levels, after price came into that level, at a useful size. Patterns that need a follow-up candle are marked pending until it comes.

Visual aid only. M3 never grades a stock and never touches the GO or an alert. It answers one question: did buyers (or sellers) show up at this level, in a shape worth noticing? Since v1.5 S4 prints that answer too (§05b).

How it reaches S4

input.source carries numbers only, so M3 sends the newest pattern as three hidden plots: s4_candleCode (the pattern, its state, age, level type and trend packed into one number), s4_candleLvl (the level price) and s4_candleTrig (the close that confirms it). S4 binds them as Add-ons: candle code / level / trigger and S4Core.candleRead turns them back into words. Only a pattern no older than 5 bars is sent (input Show on S4 for); after that the row reads no candlestick at a key level recently. tests/test_addons_m3.py keeps the name lists in the two files equal and checks the packing survives a 32-bit float.

Kept out of every grade until it has been measured.

06M3 · The three filters

Shape alone has almost no edge on stocks (see §11). Each mark must clear all three:

1

Key level

The pattern's low (bullish) or high (bearish) is within 0.5 × ATR14 of a level, and the close holds the level. Levels come from S4's own S/R engine (S4Core.srLevels, the same settings as S4) on Daily, Weekly and Monthly: the two nearest each side. Monthly outranks Weekly, Weekly outranks Daily, and a tie goes to the nearer level. A timeframe below the chart's is skipped (on a Weekly chart, Daily levels are not used). Daily EMA20, the 50-DMA and the 200-DMA can be added as extra levels; they are off by default because price touches them often.

2

Arrival

Price came into the level: a bullish pattern holds the lowest low of the last 5 bars, a bearish one the highest high. This is the "prior decline" every textbook definition asks for. A hammer in the middle of a range is not drawn.

3

Size

The whole pattern spans at least 0.6 × ATR14 from high to low. Bulkowski finds tall candles do better; a tiny candle at a level is noise.

One mark per bar. When several patterns pass on the same bar, the strongest in priority order takes the label (see the table in §08), and the others are listed in its tooltip as also. A bearish pattern is only looked for on a bar with no bullish mark.

07M3 · Primary trend

The primary trend comes from the Swing Zigzag's strict trend, bound by BIND_S4_SOURCES through two inputs: chart timeframe (the default) or weekly. A bullish pattern while the trend reads DOWN, or a bearish one while it reads UP, is counter-trend: marked ↺ and dimmed. It can also be hidden or shown in full.

Why the Zigzag trend is not the "decline" test

Inside a Stage-2 pullback the Zigzag still reads UP, and that is exactly where these patterns work best. Bulkowski finds bullish candles do best as a retrace inside a rising primary trend, and inside a falling one they reverse it only briefly. So the Zigzag sets with-trend versus counter-trend, and the separate arrival test (§06) checks that price came down into the level.

If the Zigzag inputs are not bound, M3 falls back to the 50-DMA (close against it and its 10-bar slope) and prints a quiet note in the bottom-right corner: Zigzag trend not bound — using the 50-DMA. Run BIND_S4_SOURCES.

10M3 · Reading a mark

Each label sits under the bar (bullish) or over it (bearish) and shows the short code, the status and ↺ if counter-trend. Hover it for the full read:

Hammer at Weekly S/R 1,842.50 · 3 touches
primary trend UP (Zigzag chart TF)
Bulkowski: bullish reversal 60% (random ~50%); a white body does better.
also: Tweezer bottom
PENDING - needs a close above 1,868.20 within 3 bars

A short dotted line marks the level the pattern formed on, by default for the latest pattern only. Patterns older than 150 bars are cleared.

How to use it

Treat a confirmed bullish mark at a level as evidence that buyers showed up there, the same question S4's L and B gates ask in words. It does not replace the S4 GO and it is not an entry on its own. A bearish mark at resistance on a holding is a reason to look at the Daily close, not to sell on the mark.

10bM3 · How long a mark counts

A candlestick is a timing event: one to three bars that say buyers or sellers showed up at a level. It goes stale fast, and the windows below are where it stops counting.

WindowBarsWhat happens
Confirm3A pattern that needs confirmation reads ? until a close beyond its high (bullish) or low (bearish). No such close within 3 bars turns it ✗: "no follow-through" (input Confirm within)
Shown on S45S4's Intraday │ Pattern row and its SUMMARY CANDLE section print the newest pattern only while it is at most 5 bars old (input Show on S4 for). Older reads 🕯 no candlestick at a key level recently
Drawn on the chart150History only, for looking back (input Show patterns from the last)
  • ? pending is a question until its confirming close. It is not a trigger.
  • ✓ older than 5 bars is history, even if the level is still holding. The level's own reads (S4's L and B gates, S/R tests) take over.
  • ✗ failed is information: the level did not hold that time.
  • When a candle and a chart pattern disagree, the pattern carries more weight. A chart pattern is structure that lasts as long as it took to build (Doc 30 §04c); a candle is one to three bars. On VIJAYA (27 Oct) a failed doji at 1,374.50 leaned mildly long while S5 showed a confirmed, counter-trend triple top at 1,380: the top decided it.
Bars, not days

Every age here is counted in bars of the chart it is drawn on, so read it on the trade's own clock. A positional trade is decided on the Daily close: 3 bars is about 3 sessions, 5 bars about a week. A swing is timed on 75m or 125m: 3 bars is half a day to a day, and an intraday mark older than about a day says nothing about the setup.

Not tested on this desk's data. The 3-bar confirm follows Bulkowski's confirmation-by-close; the 5-bar display window follows the PA battery, where a pattern 1–3 bars old tested no worse than one on today's bar on Daily, 125m and 75m (PREREG_pa_recency_daily / _intraday, 15–16 Oct). The candles never gate, so these are reading rules, not measured edges.

11M3 · The research behind the filters

  • Shape alone has almost no edge on stocks. Studies on Thai SET50 stocks (2017) and on 20 US stocks found most patterns' returns not different from zero. That matches this desk's own result: the PA gate times entries, it does not select them (PREREG_pa_selects). Hence the level, arrival and size filters.
  • Where they work (Bulkowski): as a retrace inside a rising primary trend; tall candles; and confirmation by a later close beyond the pattern.
  • Modern adaptations used: ATR-relative sizes instead of fixed points; no gap required for piercing line, dark cloud or the stars, because NSE stocks rarely gap between candles intraday; one label per bar by priority.
  • Left out on purpose: Bulkowski's "best near the yearly low" (it works against Stage-2 leaders near their highs), and a volume filter (the 13-August measurement showed relative volume does not separate good pullbacks from bad ones).

Sources: thepatternsite.com (Morning Star, Hammer, Bullish Engulfing pages); Bulkowski, Eight best-performing candles (S&C); "Profitability of candlestick patterns" (SAGE Open, 2017, SET50); candlestick patterns on US stocks (UCLAN).

13After an Add-ons compile

  1. Order for v1.5: Add-ons imports S4Core/73, the same version as S4. Publish S4Core first, then compile S4 and Add-ons.
  2. Run BIND_S4_SOURCES (or AFTER_COMPILE). A compile drops M3's two Zigzag inputs and S4's three candle inputs; the binder wires both. On a tab with Add-ons it reads bound 35/35 and ends add-ons M3: 2/2. On a tab without Add-ons (Panel Layout) S4's three candle inputs are skipped, not failed.
  3. An S4 compile also drops the three candle bindings, so run the binder after either compile.
  4. The binder's 8-second re-check covers S4 only. If M3's corner note appears later, run it again.
  5. AFTER_COMPILE step 5 also pushes the v2 status for M2 (tv_push_addons.py).
  6. An Add-ons compile does not touch S4's alerts. It does change the plots S4 binds, so re-run the binder.
