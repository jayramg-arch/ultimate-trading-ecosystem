Paper Trades post-mortem

Why 43 of 52 Reviewer TAKEs stopped out: stops inside normal noise, a falling tape, picks that lagged the index, a timing layer that did not select, and clustered entries. What was already fixed, and what was not.

Source: data/paper_trades.csv (cohort "Reviewer TAKE", 52 filled), logs/ai_review_log.csv, logs/gm_signal_log.csv (outcomes filled 16 Oct), the MarketRegime table, and an independent re-simulation from Dhan daily bars (reports/paper_failure_sim_20261016.csv).

The headline

Stops were the visible cause, but not the only one. Five things stacked:

The 4×ATR floor alone moves the stop-out rate from 79% to 23% — inside the 40% you said you can live with — but the result is still negative, because causes 2–4 remain. The trades taken after the floor (S4 TAKE IT cohort, n = 10) are running at −0.33 R with 6 stopped, against −0.78 R before: better, still small and still negative.

What is already fixed (all live before this post-mortem)

  • 4 × ATR(D) positional stop floor — 3 Oct.
  • Exposure rule — 6 Oct. On these 52 fill dates it would have blocked all 24 BEAR-score entries and limited the other 28 to half risk, three a week: about 9–10 trades instead of 52.
  • Open-risk budget — 7 Oct.
  • Ladder grace period, two-tier stop, Chandelier anchored at entry — 7–12 Oct.

So most of these losses come from a policy that is no longer the policy.

What is NOT fixed — and the action plan

  1. Paper Trades measures a policy you do not run. It takes every TAKE at full house size, regardless of the exposure tier, the weekly cap or the open-risk budget. Fix: the paper cohort honours the tier (BEAR = no entry, NEUTRAL = half risk, max 3 a week) and the open-risk budget, so it shows what the rules would actually have done.
  2. No baseline cohort. Add a third paper cohort — "qualified, no timing": every Stage-2 catalyst name bought at the close with the 4 × ATR stop. It answers, live, whether the GO gate and the reviewer add or subtract.
  3. GO selected against returns in this tape. Pre-register a forward test: GO rows vs non-GO rows on the live boards, by regime tier, from 17 Oct. If GO keeps trailing non-GO in BEAR/NEUTRAL tapes, GO alerts are suspended in those tiers. Not acted on now: one regime, and it is the same falling tape that hurt everything.
  4. Breakout GOs were the worst bucket (−1.27pp, 34% beat; pullbacks −0.75pp, 37%; recovery +0.37pp, 52%). Include in the same pre-registration: in non-BULL tiers, are breakout GOs worse than pullback GOs?
  5. The reviewer does not discriminate. TAKE, WAIT and PASS returned the same over 10 sessions. Keep it as the plan writer and checklist (it catches void stops, ceilings as targets, mis-stated R); stop reading TAKE as a signal. The paper cohort "Reviewer TAKE" is not a strategy and should be relabelled as a record.
  6. Concentration. A weekly cap on new entries in every tier (not only NEUTRAL), and the correlation gate on new entries as well as adds.
  7. Hold positionals as positionals. With the 4 × ATR stop, the trail (Chandelier 4.5 ×) does the work; 88% of positional exits in the backtests came off the trail. Do not tighten.

The honest bottom line

The ecosystem's only measured edge is selection by stage and catalyst — about +1% per trade against the index historically, with a confidence interval that includes zero. The timing layer (GO, the reviewer) has not shown any edge in four separate tests, and in this falling tape it picked slightly worse names. In a BEAR tape the right position is mostly cash, which the exposure rule now enforces. The work ahead is not more indicators: it is fewer, wider-stopped positional trades taken only when the tape allows, and a live baseline that tells us within a few months whether the timing layer earns its keep.
