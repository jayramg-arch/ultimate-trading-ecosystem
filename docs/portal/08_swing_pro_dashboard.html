01Timeframe scope — read before anything else

The price-action cascade was validated on the DAILY timeframe only

The dashboard renders on any chart timeframe, and the structural references — stage, moving averages, relative strength — correctly fetch from the higher timeframe wherever you put it. The candle-pattern detectors do not. They operate on whatever bars the chart is showing.

So the alpha numbers below do not transfer to intraday. An intraday breakout pattern is a fundamentally different event from a daily one, however identical the label looks. Weekly is not designed for at all — the mixed-timeframe references produce odd mismatches.

Use it: keep the price-action field on daily for setup discovery and positional decisions. For intraday entry timing, read raw price action manually rather than trusting the tier output.

What the table changed

  1. The tier-3 states are the highest-edge setups in the field — statistically airtight across thousands of trades. Stage-2 Launch at +14% over 180 days is the strongest single signal anywhere in the system.
  2. Bull Engulf became selective rather than common. Gating it to an oversold bar with institutional volume moved it from roughly zero alpha to +3.46% — the same pattern, a tenth as often, worth taking when it appears.
  3. Four bearish detectors were switched off. Regime-split validation showed all four predicted the wrong direction in both regimes. They still display, tagged watch, for human pattern recognition — but they no longer penalise the score.
  4. Co-firing bullish states now win. A bar firing both a distribution day and a pocket pivot resolves to the pocket pivot. The old priority was actively wrong per the data.
  5. Late-stage bases demote automatically. A third base drops a tier, a fourth drops two. Base one is prime; base three is late cycle.
The lesson underneath the table

Two detectors were firing zero times and one was structurally impossible — a rolling maximum that included the bar being tested, making its own condition a tautology. A detector that never fires looks identical to a detector that finds nothing. Only counting the fires distinguishes them.

Why Stage-2 age has two numbers

A single number could not answer both “how long has this leg been running” and “how old is this whole advance”. Conflating them made a mature Stage 2 look fresh after any pullback — the exact mistake that puts you into base four thinking it is base one.

07Combining the two headline cells

They answer different questions, and reading either alone is how you end up in the right stock on the wrong day.

RecommendationAction SignalInterpretation
STRONG BUY≥ 7/10Full confluence — act on the next valid pivot
STRONG BUY4–6/10Right regime, wrong day. Wait for a tighter bar
BUY≥ 7/10Acceptable entry; size down against a strong buy
HOLD / WATCHLIST≥ 7/10A high-quality bar inside a base — a pocket pivot or coil. Watchlist it with alerts
AVOID / NO TRADEanySkip regardless of bar quality
any≤ 2/10Wait. Even a clean stock has zero edge today
The distinction in one line

Recommendation says whether you should be looking at this stock. Action Signal says whether today is the day. The first is slow and structural; the second changes every bar.

08What this surface owns

Under the ownership rule, every concept has exactly one owner and everyone else mirrors it verbatim or drops the duplicate. The dashboard is the primary trade chart, so it owns the fields you read to judge a name — and deliberately mirrors the ones it does not.

CatalystMirrored verbatim from the screener — gate-validated, not re-derived

Why the sector database is physical

The platform's own sector field is unreliable for this market. A generated database of several hundred tickers takes precedence, which is what gives 100% offline parity with the Python side — and removes an entire class of string-matching bug.

11Inputs — field by field

Folded by group. Defaults are the validated values; change them only against your own data, and remember that several are shared with other surfaces.

Portfolio slots — and the one rule about them
Slots are for OPEN positions only

They are reserved for stocks you currently hold, never for watchlist tracking. Every slot metric — P&L, R-multiple, days held, time-stop warnings, trailed-stop tracking — only makes sense once a position is live with a real entry, stop and date. Put a watchlist ticker in a slot and all of them will lie to you. The sync script enforces this by pulling only open positions.

—The weekly MA plots — visible, and settable (25 Aug 2026)

Two separate causes, reported as one problem: “the 30WMA and 40WMA are not visible. Also, on the settings→Style tab, why am I not able to set the colors?”

1 · Invisible

The 40-week MA was hardcoded color.black — a black line on a near-black chart. Drawn correctly every bar and impossible to see. Now a light grey that reads on either theme.

2 · The Style tab did nothing

A colour passed as a color= argument is re-applied by the script on every bar, so whatever you pick in Style is overwritten before you see it. The swatch is there; it just never wins.

Both MAs now source input.color, so the Style tab is the script's input and your choice survives. New group Weekly MA colours: separate colours for the 30W rising and falling states (the slope conditional is preserved — both branches are yours), one for the 40W, and show/hide toggles.

The general rule

A plot whose colour is computed is not user-settable — and a conditional colour is computed by definition. If you want a plot's colour adjustable, it has to come from an input.

12Common mistakes

  1. Trusting the pattern tiers on an intraday chart. The validation is daily-only. The label is the same; the event is not.
  2. Reading the Action Signal without the Recommendation. A perfect bar in a Stage-4 name is still a Stage-4 name.
  3. Treating a watch state as a veto. Those four detectors were switched off precisely because they predicted the wrong direction. They are for your eyes, not the score.
  4. Ignoring the trend-engine sync warning. It means this cell and your pivot tool now disagree about structure.
  5. Reading Stage-2 age as one number. The leg resets on every pullback; the macro clock does not. Base four is not base one.
  6. Treating the portfolio rows as live. They are a synced snapshot.
  7. Chasing a name with no room. The room row exists because a clean setup into overhead supply is still a bad trade.
  8. Expecting a fundamental gate here. It moved to the strategy indicator; this cell is technical state only.
