01Where it actually lives now

The standalone indicator no longer exists

The markdown guide describes a separate Wyckoff Phases script with its own inputs. There is no such file. Wyckoff, Volume Profile and the zone module were merged into one context overlay, and the Wyckoff logic was later ported into the entry-trigger script and into Python. Everything below is read from those live sources, not from the retired guide.

SurfaceWhat it does with Wyckoff
The context overlayThe canonical Pine implementation — events, labels, bias, and a decaying score component that feeds the context total
The entry-trigger scriptCarries the same logic; the Python port was made against it
The context module in PythonA one-to-one port so the board and the chart agree — see section 07
The recovery screenerA separate, stricter implementation that produces actual tradeable catalysts — section 08
And the guide's detection description was wrong even for its own version

It says events are sequential and stateful — that a preliminary support must print before a climax can be labelled. The live code is not sequential. Each event is an independent test of one pivot bar's shape and volume, and collisions are resolved by a priority ladder, not by a phase state machine. That difference changes how you read a chart: you can get a sign of strength with no climax before it.

02Tested twice as a trading input, rejected twice

Before the mechanics, the evidence — because it determines what this layer is for.

ProposalResult
Use a distribution reading as a veto on the entry gateBackwards. The vetoed cohort returned +5.60% against +0.52% for the cohort it kept
Feed the Wyckoff score into the rankingNull. Held-out rank correlation +0.013, and it was null in both windows
The mechanism, and why it will not transfer

49% of already-qualified picks read DISTRIBUTION at signal time. Not because they were topping — because Wyckoff distribution events fire at high-volume pivot highs, and that is structurally what a breakout is. A wide, heavy, closing-strong bar at a new high is both a sign of strength and, one bar later at a confirmed pivot, an upthrust candidate.

The port is faithful. The concept simply does not transfer to a universe that has already been filtered for breakouts. Do not re-litigate this — it is measured, in both directions.

So what is it good for

Reading a chart, and grading what you are already looking at. It stays on the panel as context and as a decaying score component. It does not gate, it does not rank, and it should never be the reason you take or skip a trade on its own.

04How detection really works

Every event is one test, applied to a confirmed pivot bar, built from four primitives:

PrimitiveDefinition
High volumeAbove the volume average times the high multiplier
Low volumeBelow the average times the low multiplier — required by every exhaustion event
Wide / narrowRange above 1.4 times the volatility measure, or below 0.6 times it
Close positionIn the top third of the bar, or the bottom third

Three fixes worth knowing, because each removed a class of false event
FixWhat it stopped
Weakness needs a lower-low pivotIt was firing on heavy down bars inside a range — a structural breakdown has to break structure
Climax excludes up bars; strength excludes bodyless shapesThe two used to collide on the same bar. Now the close-versus-open test separates a genuine demand thrust from an exhaustion print with a long upper wick
A spring needs an upper-third close and an up barAny low-volume dip of a fraction of a percent used to qualify. A spring is a recovery, not a wobble
The label lands late, by construction

A pivot resolves on its confirmation bar, ten bars after the pivot itself at the default length. So the script tests the pivot bar's characteristics but records the event at the confirmation bar — and freshness and decay are both measured from there. An event you see today describes a bar from ten bars ago.

05The priority ladder, and the tie that goes bearish

A single pivot can satisfy several conditions. Accumulation resolves top-down, strongest first:

Load-bearing quirk — do not "clean it up"

The distribution block is a second independent test, not an alternative branch. It runs after the accumulation block and overwrites it. So a bar that satisfies both resolves to DISTRIBUTION — the bearish read always wins a tie.

Combined with section 02, this is most of the story: on a breakout bar both sides can fire, and the tie-break hands you the bearish label on a name that is doing exactly what you wanted.

06Bias, score and decay

Three values persist between events and are what the panel actually shows:

ValueMeaning
BiasAccumulation, distribution or neutral — set by the last event that fired, and it stays set until another one does
Base scoreThe event's tier, from +4 to −4
Decayed scoreWhat feeds the context total

The score fades 25% every decay step, so at the default it is worth nothing after four steps — a half-life of roughly forty-five bars. That is the mechanism that stops a months-old climax still colouring today's read.

Read bias and score together, never separately

Bias does not decay; the score does. A chart can print “accumulation” with a decayed score of zero — meaning the last thing that happened was bullish, and it happened long enough ago to be irrelevant. Bias alone is the single most misread cell on the panel.

07The Python port, and the three quirks it reproduces on purpose

Before the port, the board showed Wyckoff from proxies — accumulation was inferred from a volume flag and the stage, which is not Wyckoff at all. The port is one-to-one against the Pine so the board and the chart cannot disagree.

Reproduced deliberatelyWhy it is load-bearing
Events record at the confirmation bar while testing the pivot barFreshness and decay are measured from the confirmation bar. Change this and every age is off by the pivot length
The bearish block is a second test, so distribution wins tiesSection 05. Making it an alternative branch would silently change roughly half the labels
The structure-shift flag reads the prior bar's trendIt is evaluated before the trend updates on that bar

Because all three depend on state carried bar to bar, the port is a bar-by-bar loop rather than a vectorised pass. That is a deliberate trade of speed for parity, and the cost is negligible next to fetching the data.

Eighteen regression tests pin these three behaviours, each verified to go red under mutation. Pivot ties are treated as breaking the pivot, matching the platform.

08The recovery catalysts — the one place Wyckoff trades

Separate from the context layer, the recovery screener implements its own stricter Wyckoff and emits real catalysts. These are validated and they are the priority recovery edge.

CatalystWhat must be trueEntry
SpringWithin the recent window, dipping no more than a few percent below the base low, on volume at or under average, and closing back above the base low within a few barsAfter the rejection back into the range
Sign of strengthSpread at least 1.5 times the recent average, closing in the upper third, on at least 1.5 times average volumeThe first pullback after it
Jump across the creekA close above the range high on twice average volumeImmediately — later, but safer
Why this version outranks the older recovery edges

A spring followed by a sign of strength describes a completed structure — supply exhausted, then demand proving itself. A bounce bar describes one day. That is also why these stayed when the context-layer Wyckoff was rejected as a gate: a strict multi-bar structure on beaten-down names is a different test from a single event label on a breakout universe.

Note the inversion that makes the spring real: volume must be at or below average. A heavy break of the base low is not a spring, it is a breakdown.

09Reading a cycle

The accumulation sequence

  1. A selling climax prints. Heavy, wide, closing at the low. This is the potential bottom and it is not an entry — risk is still undefined.
  2. An automatic rally follows. Note the level; it is the top of the range.
  3. A secondary test comes back down on lighter volume. Supply is drying up. This is the first watch point, and it maps to the recovery book's climax path.
  4. A sign of strength breaks the range on expanding volume. Demand is present.
  5. A last point of support pulls back quietly into the breakout area. This is the entry the method is built around — stop below the pullback low.

The distribution warning

  1. A buying climax at the highs — start tightening on existing longs.
  2. An upthrust — a failed break above. If you are long from the base, this is an exit signal, not a reason to add.
  3. A sign of weakness — structural confirmation. Close what remains.

Everything in this section is chart-reading discipline, not a mechanical rule. The measured results in section 02 say the labels do not select winners on this universe. Use the sequence to understand what a chart is doing; take the trade on the system's own gates.

10Tuning

InputDefaultEffect
Pivot length10Bars each side to confirm a swing. Higher gives fewer, stronger events — right for positional. Lower is noisier
Volume lookback20The baseline. Raise toward 50 on very quiet names
High multiplier1.5×What counts as heavy. Higher is stricter and produces fewer events
Low multiplier0.7×What counts as dry. Lower is stricter — this is the one that governs springs and tests
Decay step15 bars25% fade per step. Lower prefers fresher setups; raise toward 30 for long holds
Module toggleonTurning it off suppresses the labels only — the score still feeds the panel
The one combination that breaks it

Keep the volume lookback at least twice the pivot length. Below that, events confirm before there is enough volume history to judge them against, and the whole layer becomes noise that still looks authoritative.

On weekly charts, shorten the pivot to five or seven, raise the decay step toward thirty, and ease the high multiplier — weekly volume is much smoother, so 1.5 times average is a rarer event than it is on a daily chart.

11Mistakes

MistakeWhy it costs you
Treating a distribution label as a sell on a breakout nameMeasured backwards — the vetoed cohort outperformed by five points. Section 02
Reading bias without the scoreBias persists; the score decays. Accumulation with a zero score means it was bullish, a long time ago
Buying the climaxThe climax is where risk is highest and undefined. The method's entry is the quiet pullback after the break, several events later
Expecting a phase sequenceDetection is per-pivot, not stateful. A sign of strength with no climax before it is normal output, not a bug
Acting on the label's barIt resolves on the confirmation bar — the event is already ten bars old at the default length
Reading events without locationAn event at the value-area low means something different from the same event mid-range. This layer was always designed to be read alongside the volume and zone layers, never alone
