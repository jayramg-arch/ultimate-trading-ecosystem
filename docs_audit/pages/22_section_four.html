01The two-stage doctrine

The board ARMS. Section Four EXECUTES. They deliberately disagree, and aligning them would be a regression.

Golden MatcherSection Four
SaysTRIGGER LIVE — a pattern firedGO — pattern and location and volume and a clean bar
FiresEarly, so you can focusLate, at the executable instant
AnswersWhich names deserve attention todayIs this the bar, and where exactly do I buy and stop
Final wordNoYes — the chart is the last authority

The board's job is to hand you a shortlist small enough to actually watch. S4's job is to stop you entering on the touch. Do not collapse them.

02Stage parity — one definition, three surfaces

A name once printed Stage 1 on the dashboard and the board, and Stage 3 on S4, for the same chart. S4 was right, and closing the gap took three separate fixes on the other side. The definition everywhere is now stateless — price against the weekly 30-SMA, multiplied by that average's slope, classified fresh each bar:

Price vs 30-WMASlopeStage
AboveRising2
AboveFalling3
AboveFlatRS up → 1, else 3
BelowRising2 — pullback
BelowFalling4
BelowFlat1
What was wrong, and why it hid so long

The other two surfaces ran a hysteresis state machine that evolved the stage from its previous value. A name trading well above a declining 30-week average never left “Stage 1” — one sat there with its average falling 62.9 points and price 5.8% above it. That is distribution, not a base. Across 56 board names, 19 (34%) were mis-staged.

Two older gaps hid underneath: the slope was a per-bar rate rather than a raw N-bar change, and the lookback was 6 against S4's 4 — together making the flat band six times too wide. Hysteresis concealed both; a stateless read exposes them immediately.

Two deliberate differences remain

The flat cell breaks its tie differently on each surface, because the tiebreaker each one has in scope differs. And Stage 2 (pullback) is named as its own cell on the dashboard while S4 folds it into Stage 2 — same family, different label granularity. Both are documented in code rather than left to be discovered.

03Reading the panel

The panel is sectioned into six bands, each stating the question its block answers. They are not interchangeable: a name can be perfect on regime and worthless on location. Read them in order and stop at the first one that fails.

I

Macro & context

What is the underlying regime? Structure basis, relative strength and rotation, sector and futures positioning, quality and momentum.

II

Location & quality

Where are we on the chart right now? The zone inventory, the support under price, nearest levels either side, volume profile, extension from the EMA20 — and the L gate.

III

Execution & timing

Are the triggers firing? The intraday read, the anchored VWAPs and their pinch, the full pattern grid with nothing aggregated away, arrival velocity — and the B and V gates.

IV

Decision synthesis

What is the ruling? The four gate chips, the STATUS line and the VERDICT. Room and confluence used to live here and were moved earlier — see the note below.

V

Plan & risk

How do we execute? Entry, stop, targets and quantity — printed only on GO.

VI

Portfolio

What do I already own here, and what does the ladder say? The v67 slot and the position state — only meaningful on a name you hold.

Two rows moved earlier on 26 Aug, and the reason is the same for both. Room for Trade sat in band IV, after the whole pattern battery — but it is a hard structural filter the verdict already treats as NOT TRADEABLE, so discovering an overhead wall after parsing seventeen tokens was wasted work. It now ends band II. Confluence moved the other way, into band III: it scores the evidence you just read, so sitting in the decision band separated it from what it summarises.

The one thing to carry away

Everything above III grades. III fires. IV rules. V sizes.

The SUMMARY column

Everything on the panel is a measurement. The SUMMARY is the reading, and it is the only field allowed to weigh one section against another. It sits in its own column down the right-hand side rather than as a row — a Pine cell does not wrap, so as a full-width row it stretched the panel across the whole screen. It never repeats a value printed above it.

Two reading traps on the panel itself

  • Two arrow families that mean different things. Diagonals are a moving-average slope; verticals are a trend state. They sit on the same row.
  • A field printing an em-dash means the source is unbound, not flat. Likewise not bound means the value bindings are missing after a recompile — not that the data is absent.

04The four gates

GO  =  P  ·  L  ·  V  ·  B

P   PATTERN    a pattern from the active battery fired on this bar
L   LOCATION   price is AT value — inside a zone, near one, or reacting off one
V   VOLUME     relative volume clears the floor
B   BAR        the bar itself is not a distribution candle

They print as chips on the TRIGGER row, plus an optional timing marker, the path tag, and any warning suffix. Timing is optional — it times, it does not qualify.

The pullback relaxation

When a contraction pattern fires with no expansion pattern and price sits inside a demand zone, the gate relaxes: the volume floor drops and the bar test becomes held the zone rather than closed strong. Tagged ·PB. Measured reason: at-value bars print a median relative volume of 0.63 against 1.35 on breakout bars, so the standard floor rejects roughly 78% of them.

Measured, and it did not support inverting the test

Across 74,267 at-value bars, the driest volume bucket was not the best performer — forward alpha rose with volume, not against it. So the relaxation exists to stop discarding a mildly positive population, not because dry volume is better. Do not reason from it in the other direction.

The sources feed the L gate in descending authority — and which KIND of zone matters as much as which source, which is what rule A2 below is about:

And a zone does not have to be untouched to count: price reacting off one it has already tested is the tradeable state. The primary source

Three states, not one — and only two of them are location

Price does not have to be inside a zone. That was the gate's definition until 26 August and it was the wrong question: the tradeable moment is the reaction — price retraces into the zone, tests it once, and turns back out. The engine had been computing that state all along and the gate was not reading it.

StateWhat it meansSatisfies L?
INPrice is inside the zone right nowYes
REACTINGTested once and turning up off it, and not yet far awayYes
APPROACHINGDescending toward an untested zone, within one ATR of itNo

"Not too far away" is not a new threshold. A reaction stays open only until price travels two ATR from the proximal, or crosses the reference EMA20 — the same rule that retires the zone. So the gate reads that rule back rather than restating it, which is why the two can never disagree.

APPROACHING is shown and deliberately not honoured. Nothing has reacted yet, so passing it would be buying the touch — the one habit the whole two-stage design exists to prevent. It earns a name on the panel so you can watch it; it does not arm anything.

Rule A2 — a pivot shelf is not a leg-base-leg zone

Two things get drawn as "zones" and they are not equal evidence. A pattern zone (RBR / DBR / RBD / DBD) marks where an imbalance actually happened and has a distal edge that defines where you are wrong. A pivot shelf marks where price merely turned once. The gate treated them identically for months while this file's own header called the pivot "a weaker secondary shelf — location/stop confluence, not standalone".

Under rule A2 a pattern zone stands alone; a pivot shelf must be confirmed by an S/R level or an anchored VWAP. Pivot levels feed the gate too — a pivot line, and a "Pivot S→R" once price has reclaimed it — and they enter as pivot evidence, so A2 still applies to them.

The master switch. Turning pivot zones off removes them everywhere: no pivot zone is drawn, no pivot satisfies location, the pivot support lines are hidden, and pivots stop counting as overhead in Room, T1 and T2. That last part is a deliberate choice of Jay's over a documented objection — a hidden ceiling is not a cleared one, so R:R will read better with pivots off than the chart justifies. Measured on the live board, switching them off takes location from roughly 44% of names to 24%.

Measured, so it is not a preference dressed as a finding: a pre-registered A/B across eighteen anchors compared any-zone, A2 and pattern-only. All three landed within 0.21 percentage points of each other on matched alpha — a null. What the tightening reliably changed was the number of trades: 221, then 88, then 51. Pattern-only is not better per trade; it is rarer. A2 is shipped because the evidence cannot separate them, so the choice falls back on which evidence you trust.

Why a GO can still be a chase

A GO resting only on an anchored VWAP or the EMA20 — no zone, no shelf — is downgraded to CAUTION — momentum/chase. That is a momentum entry, not a pullback to support. It is also exactly why the board can read “no location” while S4 shows a trigger: the board's Python twin deliberately excludes the EMA20, because it fires most of the time and would make the board over-predict.

Repeated tests WEAKEN a level

Heavily-tested levels are deliberately excluded from the nearest-level picker. A level tested that often is a breakout candidate, not a ceiling. When nothing is found above, that is what produces BLUE SKY.

07The verdict ladder

Evaluated in order — the first match wins. This is the output that matters; everything above it exists to feed it.

No trade — Stage 3/4
A valid trigger inside an invalid context. Applies under manual mode too — the stage is a fact, not a preference.
Take it — recovery
A recovery climbs through overhead, so thin room is expected and does not deny the trade. Buy-stop confirms the turn — overrides your entry method.
Breakout pivot
Stage 2, near the high, sitting in a supply band — because the supply IS the prior high. Don't buy here; arm above the band. Blue sky above it.
Pullback to value
The house A+ location. Stage 2, in a demand zone, not extended, not in supply. Keeps your retest default — which is the entire point of it.
Clear to break
A level overhead that is there to be broken, not targeted. Can only ever reframe a would-be skip.
Skip — no room
In supply, or under 1R of room. Buying into overhead supply is not a trade.
Take it / caution
A reward gate clears. Caution when location is weak or price is extended.
Skip — payoff thin
Entry is clean; the reward does not justify the risk.

Below a GO the ladder continues with NOT TRADEABLE — blocked and in supply, so clearing the one missing gate still would not make it a trade — plus LOW QUALITY and ARM, which names the single gate you are waiting on.

Which reward gate applies is decided by the trade TYPE

Not by whichever happens to pass. Swing takes the 2R bar; positional takes the ROI bar. They are not peers — swing is 5–8% over 8–12 weeks, positional 10–30% over 6–8 months.

A structural tension, stated rather than hidden

A positional plan targets 5R, so ROI-to-T1 is five times the risk percentage. With a 2.3% stop that is 11.5% — meaning the 20% positional rule is unreachable whenever risk is under 4%. The two positional rules contradict each other by construction. Either the target or the gate should move; until then the verdict explains the conflict instead of pretending it away.

08The plan

The plan row prints only on GO, and it is latched to the bar that fired. That latch matters more than it sounds: a plan that recomputes while the signal stays true ratchets the entry up with price, and a price-based staleness check will certify it as fresh precisely when it is most stale. The row tags how many bars ago the trigger fired, and re-latches rather than quoting fiction once that is too old.

FieldHow it is derived
EntryFollows your entry method — default retest, a limit at the trigger bar's close
StopStructural: zone distal → recent swing low → an ATR fallback, capped by trade type
T1 · T2R-multiples set by trade type
QuantityDynamic risk, ported from the allocator
Retest versus buy-stop — and the honest caveat

Retest is the measured default: a limit at the trigger bar's close beat the buy-stop across every family. But the simulation cannot model the false-breakout whipsaw that a buy-stop avoids, so treat that edge as optimistic rather than settled. Buy-stop above the bar's high is house doctrine and costs a higher entry to avoid the false break.

Two rulings deliberately override your setting and say so on the panel — recovery (you confirm the turn, never buy the touch of a falling name) and blue-sky (a limit would fill you inside resistance). Pullback-to-value does not override, because keeping the retest is the whole point of that ruling.

After the fill

Place the stop order after the fill — never a resting order at the level, which is how you get tagged and filled into a failing setup. From that point S4's job is over and management takes over.

Imported from the dashboard — and why the rows go blank

These bind to hidden numeric plots on the dashboard and the pivot tool. They carry the value that indicator computed — not a re-implementation — so an imported field cannot drift.

Two constraints worth knowing before you debug

A source input carries one float series, so strings cannot cross a binding. The sector name is the exception that proves the rule: it does not travel over the binding channel at all — the curated mapping is compiled into the shared library as a packed string, so S4 spends one call rather than several hundred comparisons, and a string literal costs one token however long it is. And inputs are matched by position, so inserting a new input mid-list drops every existing binding. If the rows go blank after an upgrade, re-bind rather than debug.

Unbound is safe: an unwired source defaults to a price series, every row range-checks it, and the panel prints not bound rather than silently showing dashes.

10Setting the alerts

Do this after EVERY compile

A recompile does not stale an alert — it deletes it. Alerts bind to the compiled script's id at creation, so the whole set ceases to exist the moment you compile. Two alerts went quiet for a month for exactly this reason and looked perfectly healthy in the list the entire time.

The five conditions, and which one you actually want

ConditionWhat it meansUse
S4 GOAll four gates aligned on a closed barThis is the one
AVWAP triggerA bounce or reclaim — timing only, no location or volume gateA name you are stalking
Intraday triggerRising-EMA reclaim plus a squeeze releaseNoisier; optional
AVWAP pinchThe three anchors convergingRare, high-conviction
Any PA patternAny battery pattern firedFar too loose for a watchlist — it will spam you

A · The per-symbol alert

The chart's symbol and timeframe are both inherited by the alert, so set them first.

  1. Open the chart on the symbol, on the timeframe the board row came from, with S4 loaded.
  2. Open the alert dialog.
  3. Condition, first dropdown → the Section Four indicator. Select the indicator, not the symbol — this is the step people get wrong.
  4. Second dropdown → S4 GO.
  5. Trigger → once per bar close.
  6. Expiration as far out as the plan allows, or open-ended.
  7. Notifications → app push, so it reaches your phone.
  8. Name it S4 GO · SYMBOL · 75m — symbol and timeframe in the name, so the notification tells you what to open without guessing.
  9. Create.

The dropdowns remember the last selection, so each further name is just: change symbol → open the dialog → confirm → create.

B · The watchlist alert — one alert for the whole shortlist

Supported, and the better default. The GO condition is entirely self-computed — the battery, the location test, relative volume and the bar test are all native.

Why this works when it looks like it shouldn't

Nothing in the GO gate reads a dashboard binding. That is what would otherwise have broken it — those bindings are per-chart and cannot follow a watchlist alert across symbols. The panel's context rows do depend on them, but the panel is not what the alert evaluates.

Set it up exactly as above, choosing the watchlist instead of a symbol at step 1. The ticker is already in the message, so the notification names which symbol fired.

Three caveats, all real

  1. Stage does not gate the alert. The stage check drives the verdict, not the GO condition — so a Stage-3/4 name on the watchlist can still ping. Open the panel and it will say NO TRADE. The alert is a prompt to look, not a verdict.
  2. The pasted board lists freeze at creation. Pullback context and the Bull/Recovery resolution go stale as the board changes. A name that becomes a pullback next week will not get the relaxed volume floor until the alert is recreated.
  3. The watchlist must stay in sync with the board. An alert on a stale watchlist is watching last week's shortlist.

The daily ritual — because the watchlist name carries the date

The pipeline writes a new, date-stamped watchlist every weekday after the close, so yesterday's alert points at yesterday's list by construction. Each evening:

  1. Check the board's source-issue strip first. On a thin pipeline day — one scan returned a single name recently, and header-only files have happened — you would otherwise arm a near-empty watchlist and lose a day of coverage without noticing.
  2. Delete yesterday's alert. Left in place it keeps firing on names that have already dropped out — the exact noise the shortlist exists to remove — and it eats your alert quota.
  3. Create today's on the new watchlist, named to match it — so a stale one is obvious at a glance in the alerts panel.
  4. Repeat on the second timeframe. An alert inherits the chart's resolution, so this is a separate alert created from a 125-minute chart.
Recreating daily also fixes caveat 2

The frozen board lists were the real weakness of the watchlist form. Recreating daily means they are never more than one session old.

Why both timeframes, and what actually differs

TimeframeCloses
75-minute10:30 · 11:45 · 13:00 · 14:15 · 15:30
125-minute11:20 · 13:25 · 15:30

125 is not merely the same signals, slower. Its first close is 11:20 — after the opening drive, whereas 75's first close is the opening drive: inflated volume, patterns firing in bulk, gaps unresolved. On one measured session, nine of fourteen alerts came from that first bar. So the 125 set structurally skips the noisiest bar of the day, at the cost of two fewer windows and a later entry.

Two pings on one name is ONE setup seen twice

Same engine, same gates, same bars — only the sampling rate differs, and both timeframes share the closing bar, so duplicates cluster there. Do not let a second ping raise conviction. Check the panel on the timeframe you intend to act on and treat the other as an echo.

What does NOT need recreating

The names you are stalking — arm them. An armed name is injected into the board carrying the archetypes it had when armed, so it survives into every subsequent watchlist even after it stops qualifying — which is precisely when losing the alert would hurt most.

C · Verify before relying on it

Open the alerts panel and check, on at least one alert:

  • the condition names the current script version, not an older title;
  • the resolution reads 75 or 125, not daily;
  • the trigger is once per bar close.

An alert bound to a stale script id sits in the list looking perfectly healthy and never fires. This check takes ten seconds and is the only thing that catches it.

D · What to do when one fires

Act on the ping, never on the touch. The alert exists so you do not have to watch the name tick by tick — and watching is exactly how the confirmation rule gets broken. When it fires: open S4 on that timeframe and run the pre-trade review. And remember what the alert actually asserts — a trigger fired, not that the trade is sound. The setup can be clean while the plan is not.

11The one gate that expires

Three of the four gates are fixed at the trigger bar. Location is not.

GateNatureStill true an hour later?
P · patternStructural — a pattern formedYes
V · volumeA property of that barYes
B · barA property of that barYes
L · locationPrice is at a level right nowNo — price moves off it

So an alert can fire correctly at 10:30 and the panel can read no location when you open it at 11:45. Neither is wrong — you are reading a snapshot after the event. There is a second mechanism too, though it is gentler than it used to be: a demand zone that spends its touch budget is now kept and greyed rather than deleted, so you can still see where price reacted — it simply stops arming a trade. A normal zone gets one test; a controlling or high-scoring one earns two.

Observed, not theorised

On one session nine alerts fired on the first bar. By the time the panels were read, two showed no location while the other seven still had all four. One still read P✓ L· V✓ B✓ with its anchor pressed within four rupees of price — it had simply drifted off the level.

What to do about it

The alert marks a BAR, not a state. Arriving late, re-evaluate at the current bar — if location has gone, the entry premise has gone with it, because the trade was “buy at the level” and price is no longer at the level. This is also exactly why the plan latches the trigger bar rather than re-deriving the entry from live price.

A timing note worth carrying

The first close of the session carries the opening drive — volume is inflated and patterns fire in bulk. A cluster of alerts there is expected, and deserves more scrutiny than a midday trigger, not less.

12Quality ladder — when several names ping

A+
Take it — pullback to value. A fresh demand zone under price, Σ ≥ 4, strong volume, arrival not grinding. The house setup.
A
Take it with zone or shelf location, both reward gates comfortable, clean bar.
A−
Breakout pivot on a Stage-2 leader at its high — but you buy above the band, never inside it.
B
Clear to break. Clean trigger, nearby overhead, Stage 2. The real target is the R-objective, not the level.
B−
GO but location is AVWAP or EMA only → the panel says caution, momentum/chase. Size down, or wait for a zone. A pivot shelf alone does not even get here — under rule A2 it fails the gate outright unless a second source agrees.
Wait
ARM or 3/4 — one gate short. Coils are compression, not a trigger.
Skip
No room · payoff too thin · low quality (grinding arrival with negative delta).
Never
No trade — Stage 3/4, and not tradeable — the second means clearing the blocking gate alone still would not make it a trade.

13Troubleshooting

SymptomCause and fix
“Too many tokens”You are at the compiled ceiling. Every addition needs a paired removal, and cost is per operation, not per character — a long string literal is one token
“Calculation timed out”, only after the openExecution time is Pine's fourth ceiling, and the only one that does not announce itself at compile. Something is running per tick rather than per closed bar
A GO alert never fired, though the panel showed GOThe alert was destroyed by a recompile — they bind to the script id. Delete and re-create every alert after every compile
GO never appears on strong triggersLocation — price is not at a zone or level. Check the Support Zone row. If intraday zones are missing entirely, the per-timeframe width band is the first knob
Intraday zones vanish, or too few daily onesPer-timeframe leg-in strictness. A single global value starves one end — that is why there are separate intraday and higher-timeframe settings
A weekly zone disappeared for no reasonThe old fix was to restrict the EMA and pivot rules to daily zones. That was the wrong lever: the defect was the wrong series — the daily average adjudicating a weekly base, at daily granularity. Both rules now run on every timeframe, each zone against its own reference. Measured across thirty names, weekly survival changed by 2.3% and monthly not at all
A heavily-tested level is not offered as supportWorking as designed. Tests weaken a level; one tested that often is a breakout candidate, not a ceiling
Verdict says NO TRADE but the chart looks fineStage 3 or 4. The stage check outranks everything and applies under manual mode too. Trust it, or accept it is a counter-trend trade at half size
Recovery mode says TAKE IT on what looks like a bull pullbackA repaired Stage-2 name now falls through to the bull rulings and the panel says the mode is contradicted. On an older build, set Mode manually
The panel gives two different entry pricesFixed. Only recovery and blue-sky override your entry method, and both say so explicitly on the panel
“Retest” filled at marketCorrect, and now stated. On the trigger bar itself the limit is that bar's close. The panel distinguishes a true retest from a market fill and names where a real pullback sits
Board says 4/4 but S4 shows nothingCheck three things in order: mode and path match; pattern age — a recency tag means it fired bars ago, so the live bar is legitimately quiet; and board snapshot staleness
Fields read not boundThe bindings dropped on the last recompile. They bind by position, and an unbound source silently reads price rather than erroring. Re-bind rather than debug
Pattern rows all dashes on a fresh addGive the higher-timeframe requests a moment, and confirm the master detect toggle is on
The panel stops after a rowA stale persisted table from an older build. Reload the current file into a fresh chart
A number prints as 1.2 when the true value is 0.94Pine's short format strings are broken — they round to an integer and print a literal decimal. Use the explicit two-place form
The Shape half always reads an em-dashWorking as designed since v7.3. The geometry classifier was removed to fund the pullback-aware gate; it lives in the S/R + Trendline Lab. Its old rectangle-as-triangle defect can no longer occur here
The one rule

Never buy the touch. Confirmation is the mechanical fix for the single most expensive habit in this system — entering at the zone because it looks right. The alert pings on a closed bar; read the verdict; enter per the plan row.
