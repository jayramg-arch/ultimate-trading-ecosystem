Restyling the Commander

The Library looks coherent because every page is built from tokens and a handful of semantic classes. The Commander already has that system — it just isn’t being used. This is what it would take to close the gap, on a teal palette chosen for a screen you sit in front of all day — and the part that cannot be copied across.

The finding

01The design system exists. Almost nothing uses it.

I expected the obstacle to be Streamlit. It isn’t. weinstein_commander_web_v4.0.py already defines 35 semantic classes in a 430-line stylesheet — the same kind of vocabulary the Library runs on. Then the pages bypass them.

This is why it looks inconsistent, and it is also the good news

Seven of the most useful classes have never been used once. Every status pill, every callout, every coloured number in the app is instead written as an inline style= attribute — 338 of them, drawing on roughly 900 hardcoded hex values. Nothing enforces that two “bullish greens” on different pages are the same green, and they frequently are not.

The good news is the shape of the work. Because those classes are unclaimed, adopting them is additive — you are not rewriting a system, you are starting to use one that is already there and already loaded.

What actually carries over

02Tokens, type, and a dark theme — not the layout

The Library is a reading document: 1180px column, generous whitespace, one idea per band. The Commander is an operational dashboard at full width, scanned rather than read. Copying the Library’s layout would make the Commander worse — you would fit a third as much on screen during a live session.

What changed, and why

The gradient and the glow go. Three blue stops plus a coloured drop-shadow on every cell is decoration competing with the numbers — and in a book with a red position, the cell tinting fights the P&L colour. Flat surfaces divided by hairlines read faster and leave colour free to mean something: the left border now carries direction.

The title stops shouting. Rajdhani 800 at 3px letter-spacing in caps is a gaming-dashboard convention; it costs width and reads slower. Sentence case in a grotesque at a smaller size gives the same authority and lets the strip sit closer.

Numbers get tabular figures. font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums — digits align in columns and stop jittering as they tick. On a live P&L strip this is the single highest-value line of CSS in the whole proposal.

Same meanings, lower volume — and a correction to what I said last round

Every hue keeps its job. Green is still bull, red still bear, amber still warn. The learned association you read before you read the number is untouched, and nothing has been swapped for anything else.

What changed is saturation. Last round I argued for keeping the semantic colours exactly. That was over-cautious. #DC2626 is a fire-engine red built to alarm — correct for a smoke detector, wrong for a screen you sit in front of from pre-market to the evening arming session. On a book with several red rows it is genuinely tiring. #C2453C says the same thing at a lower volume. Softening a hue is a different act from swapping one, and only the first is safe.

The neutrals now carry a teal bias rather than Tailwind’s stock slate ramp (#1E293B, #334155, #94A3B8, #CBD5E1) — a blue-green grey that sits under the accent instead of competing with it. Teal replaces the #1D4ED8 accent outright: it is the calmest hue that still reads as “interactive”, and unlike blue it does not collide with the bear red across the colour wheel.

All four semantic colours clear 4.5:1 against their own tinted backgrounds in both themes.

Dark mode

05The thing tokens give you almost free

The Commander has no dark theme at all — prefers-color-scheme appears nowhere in 18,733 lines, and the ground is hardcoded #F0F4F8. For a screen open from pre-market through to the evening arming session, that is a real ergonomic cost.

Once colours come from tokens, dark mode is one extra block redefining the same names. This page is proof — switch your system theme and every specimen above follows, including the semantic greens and reds, which are lifted for contrast on a dark ground rather than naively inverted.

The one caveat

Streamlit’s own widgets do not follow your tokens. Getting a genuinely dark app means setting Streamlit’s base="dark" theme in config.toml as well — the tokens handle everything you render, the config handles everything Streamlit renders. Both are needed, and neither is hard.

Recommendation

06How I would actually do it

Not as a redesign. As a token migration, in phases, each one shippable and reversible — and none of them touching signal logic.

01 Extract the token layer One commander_theme.py holding the palette, the type scale and the dark block, injected once at startup. Nothing else changes yet — the app looks identical, and every subsequent phase gets cheaper. ~2h
02 Point the existing 35 classes at the tokens The stylesheet stops carrying literal hex values. This alone re-themes every page-title and metric-card — 40 call sites — and turns dark mode on for all of them at once. ~2h
03 Adopt the seven unused classes Convert inline pills and callouts to pill-* and card-*. Highest ratio of consistency gained to risk taken, because the classes already exist and are already tested. Do it page by page. ~1 day
04 Retire the inline styles, by page The long tail — 338 attributes. Strictly per page, so a bad render is always one page and one revert. Start with Risk Shield and the Dashboard: most looked-at, most numbers, most to gain. phased
05 Theme AG-Grid separately The Trigger Board is themed through AG-Grid’s own API, not CSS. Own piece of work, own risk. Last, and only if the rest lands well. optional
What I would not do

Not a big-bang restyle. A half-migrated app looks worse than a consistently dated one, because the eye reads the mismatch as breakage. Every phase above leaves the app coherent.

Not during market hours, and not bundled with anything that touches signal generation. This is presentation only — it should never appear in the same commit as a gate, a threshold or a screener change.

Not without your eye on it first. Which is why this is a mockup and not a pull request.
