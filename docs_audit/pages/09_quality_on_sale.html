01Six corrections, verified against the code

This guide has drifted further than any other in the library. Every item below was checked at source while this page was written.

1 · The fundamental gate is 5, not 1 — and it is the best-evidenced change in the repo

The guide describes a minimum of 1. The engine requires 5 of 6. That was raised on measurement, not judgement, and the measurement is the single strongest piece of evidence anywhere in this system — see the last section.

2 · The drawdown test is a BAND, not a floor

The guide says at least 25% off the high. The engine requires 15% to 35%. Deeper than 35% is not a better bargain — it is a falling knife, or trouble the market can see and you cannot. This is the change that defines the whole screener.

3 · The regime gate has an upper bound too

The guide documents only the 10% lower bound. There is also a 40% cap — beyond that a name is distressed rather than discounted, and it is excluded regardless of how good the trailing fundamentals look.

4 · Breakout volume is 1.25×, not 1.5×

The stricter figure was measured as the dominant killer of the early-recovery catalysts — it cut the survivor chain to roughly 2%. Early-recovery breakouts often fire on moderate volume; 1.25 is still a real increase without demanding a full breakout pop.

5 · The stop fallback is 2.5× ATR

Widened from the swing-sized multiple. A three-month horizon cannot be defended with a swing stop — that was throwing the edge away on ordinary noise.

6 · The recovery book HAS been validated — and the board still says it hasn't

The guide says a properly-windowed validation shows a small positive alpha “requiring more anchors”. In fact a full re-baseline completed: 400 trades across 19 anchors, with the forward windows verified at 90 and 120 days, so it clears the window rule that invalidated every earlier attempt.

But the Trigger Board still tags every recovery row as unvalidated. That flag is now stale — the run exists, it is correctly windowed, and it is what drove the gate change. Worth clearing, though that is a behaviour change and your call.

02The band — the idea everything rests on

under 15% off
not beaten down enough
15% – 35%
QUALITY ON SALE
over 35% off
falling knife

A recovery candidate must be beaten down enough for the discount to matter, and not so far that the decline is telling you something. A shallow dip is just a pullback on a healthy name — that is the bull screener's job. A collapse is usually the market pricing in information you do not have.

The regime gate applies the same logic one level up: a stock qualifies when the market has corrected meaningfully, or when the stock itself sits in a 10–40% correction band — with the upper bound doing the same work, excluding the distressed rather than the discounted.

And the fundamental floor is what makes it "quality"

Price says on sale. Only the fundamental score says quality — and it is a hard gate, not a ranking input. Without it this screener is just a list of things that fell a lot.

When no signal is active

The row prints SL/T1/T2 — (no active setup). It used to show a generic structural anchor with fallback targets — which made an untriggered watch candidate look tradeable. In that state the screener's purpose is the watch guide, not the levels.

For a live Wyckoff entry, prefer the tighter base-low stop shown by the strategy indicator over the generic recent-low anchor.

Known gap — the target on this row is not the target on the order

The row plans T1 at 2.5R. The exit policy that governs recovery positions once you are in one plans 3R and 5R, taking 25% at each. Same family, two numbers, two modules — the screener sets the level you read, the risk layer sets the legs that actually rest at the broker.

At the order, the exit policy wins. Treat the 2.5R here as what it is: a reward-plausibility check at screening time, used to grade R:R and to reject setups with no room. It is not the target you place.

The gap is deliberate for now rather than overlooked — 2.5 sits inside the screener's signal output, so moving it changes what the pending recovery re-baseline measures. Reconciling the two is a decision to take with that run's result in hand, not before it.

08Alerts and screener use

AlertFires when
Capitulation detectedThe climax bar prints — advance warning, not an entry
Capitulation bottomThe turn bar confirms and the structure completes
Survivor breakoutThe relative-strength breakout fires
Early reclaimThe compression reclaim fires

Set them once per bar close — intra-bar firing on a recovery setup is especially noisy, because the whole premise is a bar that closed a certain way.

Using it as a screener

  1. Add the indicator to any chart.
  2. Open the screener panel and add its signal and score outputs as columns.
  3. Filter to signals at capitulation-bottom or better to see live candidates.
  4. Sort by score descending.
  5. Export for offline filtering.

It works across any watchlist without API calls — but remember the table itself stays on the chart, so the screener tab narrows and the chart explains.

Wyckoff — the priority edge

All four are positional in character — a base breakout runs far when the accumulation behind it is real — and all four target a large multiple of risk rather than a nearby level. Read them in conviction order:

SignalConviction
Spring + strengthHighest. The shakeout and its confirmation on one base
Jump across the creekA decisive close above the base high on volume
Strength aloneLower — no spring on this base, so the shakeout never happened
Spring aloneEarliest — the shakeout without confirmation yet

Why Wyckoff outranks the legacy codes: a spring plus a sign of strength describes a completed structure — supply exhausted, then demand proving itself. A bounce bar describes one day.

10Which engine to run

EngineWhen
The main screener (default)The canonical recovery engine, and what the pipeline runs. Picks arrive labelled with the legacy catalyst codes
The Wyckoff variantRun in parallel when you want explicit spring, strength and creek-crossing recognition
They are additive, not alternatives

Wyckoff is a base-pattern engine; the legacy set is catalyst-triggered. The Wyckoff layer was added alongside the legacy edges, never as a replacement — a rollback that had to be made explicitly after an earlier retirement verdict was withdrawn.

The chart version is deliberately weaker on fundamentals

The chart screener runs a reduced fundamental check because the platform caps how many financial calls a script may make. Python carries the real gate. That difference is intentional and documented — but it means a name can look fine on the chart and fail the actual floor. Trade recovery off the Python output.

Pitfalls
  • It is a snapshot. Re-run on the day you intend to enter — a recovery candidate's levels age fast.
  • Signal age matters more here than on the bull side. A capitulation turn bar is a specific bar, not a state that persists.
  • A low fundamental score is a reject, not a discount. It is the gate that separates this from a list of things that fell a lot.
  • The chart table only renders on the main chart, not in the screener tab — that tab shows column values only.

13Common mistakes

  1. Using it as a standalone trigger. It is a discovery tool — validate before entering.
  2. Acting on the watch state. A climax without a turn bar is not a trade, however complete the structure looks.
  3. Ignoring a failing grade. A low score means several gates failed — no chart pattern overrides that.
  4. Mismatching the pivot setting against the pivot tool, which makes the trend gate produce inconsistent results across surfaces.
  5. Entering from a stale file. Re-run fresh on the day.
  6. Trading recovery in a bull tape. The edge is regime-fitted — in a strong market these setups are usually just ordinary pullbacks wearing a recovery label.
  7. Reading a chart-side fundamental pass as the real one. The chart check is deliberately reduced; Python carries the gate.

14Where the evidence sits

The recovery re-baseline completed: 400 trades across 19 anchors, with forward windows verified at 90 and 120 days — so it clears the window rule that invalidated every earlier attempt at measuring this book.

The book-level number is negative

Across all 400 trades, mean matched alpha is −0.88%. Taken whole, the recovery book lost over that period. That is the honest headline — and it is why the next table matters.

Why this is the best-evidenced gate change in the repo
  • The confidence interval, blocked by symbol, is [+0.92, +3.88] — it excludes zero.
  • It strengthens out of sample rather than decaying: +1.01pp in-sample becomes +4.52pp out-of-sample. That is the opposite of an overfit.
  • The high cohort beats the low in 14 of 19 anchors — not one lucky stretch.
  • Nearly all of the book's loss sat in the bucket the old floor admitted.

So the floor moved to 5, and it is the reason the current book is positive where the raw measurement is not. The gate is doing the work — which is exactly why it is a hard gate rather than a ranking input.

And the regime caveat still holds

Recovery is regime-fitted: positive in falling and recovering tapes, negative in strong up-tapes. That is correct behaviour, not a defect — do not run this book in a bull market and then conclude it does not work.
