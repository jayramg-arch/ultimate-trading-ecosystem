Note the sequencing note was followed

Return-on-equity and leverage were added as separate hard gates beside the growth score, not folded into it. That was deliberate: fold them in and the meaning of “four of five” silently changes, and each leg’s contribution stops being readable in the log.

And the lesson that came out of shipping it

The growth gate had to be closed in three places before a name that should fail actually failed — the engine, the inherited-qualification branch that reassigned the flag twenty lines later, and the renderer, which replaced the whole step with a hard-coded pass for inherited names. Each fix looked complete alone. A gate is not live until you have watched the failing case fail — pick that case first, not a passing one.

PyramidHoldings the auto-pilot rated ADDNone — you already own it
The fifth book is the one that proves the rule

Pyramid has no fundamental gate, and should not have one. An add is not a new idea — the name passed its screens when you first bought it, and re-running that test today would either wave through what you already own or, worse, veto an add on a fundamental reading you did not act on at entry.

What replaces the gate is the ladder: a holding only appears here when ADD is its classification, which already requires a leader at a genuine pullback rather than an extended one. The break-down guard still applies — arguably harder here than anywhere, since a structural break on a name you hold is a reason to sell, not to buy more.

All five qualify; the board only times. The question this document asks is not whether a book is gated but whether the gate matches the question that book is asking.

What the matrix says

  1. The bull standard is a growth standard, and it is the strictest in the system. Sales, profit, return on capital, return on equity, low debt, promoter skin in the game, near-zero pledge, a size floor — every condition joined by AND.
  2. The catalyst book used to check no growth parameter at all. Its gate was a survival test. A small-cap with flat sales, heavily pledged promoter holding and single-digit returns passed it, while every bull screen rejected the same name outright. That is the finding this document was written for, and it has since been fixed.
  3. The pullback book was the weakest of the four. Its gate asked the right kind of question but required only two of five checks — so a name could pass on “profitable and margin expanded” with negative sales and profit growth. Also fixed.
  4. Recovery is the most gated book and is internally coherent. Its saved screens are the survival checks near-verbatim, and then the engine applies the same test again, stricter. Nothing about it is relaxed.
  5. The remaining gap is ownership and size. Pledge, promoter and institutional holding and the market-cap floor are still bull-only.
Why the two weak gates happened

Both bull-family sources were built to widen supply after the join-based funnel proved too narrow. Widening the universe was the intent. Weakening the fundamental standard was a side effect, and it happened without anyone deciding it. That is the failure mode this map exists to catch.

The band collision — still open

The survivor screen demands the stock be less than 15% off its high, while its technical partner demands it be more than 10% off. The two agree only inside a narrow five-point band. The early-bird pair has the same structure at ten to twenty.

This is why the recovery joins are so lossy — survivors kept ten names out of fifty-one on one measured day. That is not fundamental strictness doing the work. It is two halves of the same filter disagreeing about which window they are looking at, for no stated reason.

Split at four: the high side returns +1.12% at 34% win against −0.19% at 27% — an edge of +1.31 points. Out of sample it retains 80% of the in-sample margin. Per anchor the high side wins eleven of seventeen.

The decision — keep the gate at four

For: the edge persists out of sample at 80% retention, clearing the bar that has killed most previous additions. And it is a threshold effect, not a gradient — one, two and three are indistinguishable and then four and five jump. That is the shape a gate should have, which is why the near-zero rank correlation is not the indictment it looks like. The portfolio-level gap is wider than the trade-level one.

Against — and this is not a footnote

The confidence interval straddles zero, and the gate is materially weaker than the survival gate it replaced on the recovery side.

Do not raise it to five — the cell holds 28 trades. And every bucket’s median is negative. The gate shifts the mean; it does not change the big-winner-carried shape of the book.

The coverage caveat

Resolution is complete across the earlier window but falls to 30–85% later — so the out-of-sample half is the thinner one, and the persistence claim rests on a partially sampled cohort. A re-check also hit network failures, so recent-anchor loss is part missing history and part infrastructure, and the two are not cleanly separable.

08The low-volume pullback branch

The question. A relative volume of 0.61 vetoed a trade. Volume is normally low on a pullback — so why does the volume floor apply at all? Mechanically, the floor drops from 1.0 to 0.5 only when a contraction pattern fires, no expansion pattern fires, and price sits inside a demand zone. That name read between zones, so the branch never engaged.

The proposal was to widen it: a pullback to a rising short average, or onto a tested level, is a real pullback too. Sixty-three thousand at-value bars, three years, twenty-session horizon, location labelled by the same engines the gate uses, bootstrapped by symbol.

Levels are clearly rejected — 1.16 points below the zone branch, interval entirely negative. The short average is rejected narrowly — 0.42 points below, interval only just excluding zero, and the cell is still positive. Widening there would dilute, not poison. If more pullback supply is ever wanted at a weaker average, that is the defensible place to take it. Levels are not.

09The anomaly, which cuts against the gate’s own rationale

Open

“None of them” is the best cell in the table, and it should be the worst.

That category means at value but not in a demand zone, not near the short average, and not near a level — half to one range out, in clear space. If being at structure is what makes a quiet at-value bar tradeable, the residual category should underperform every labelled one. It beats the demand zone by 1.35 points.

So what the study actually supports is narrow: among the location labels, the demand zone is the best one. It does not establish that being at structure is what generates the alpha. Two readings, neither tested:

  1. The residual selects for cleanly trending names that never pull back into structure at all — the label is a proxy for trend quality, not for location.
  2. The taxonomy is too coarse, and “near a level” is capturing overhead supply as often as support.
ConsequenceWhat to do about it
Do not cite this as proof that location gating worksIt compares labels. It does not validate the concept
The zone flag on the board is still offThis result is a reason to run that comparison properly, not to assume zones improve the board
The cell is smaller but not smallFour thousand bars against twenty-three thousand

Next test if this is picked up: partition the residual by trend strength — index, relative strength, distance above the long average — and see whether it is trend quality wearing a location label. Until then it is an anomaly, not a finding.

10The proposed table

The ruling that set the trade-off

“I’m ok to have fewer results, rather than a huge list of unreliable results.” An earlier draft proposed loosened common thresholds to protect supply. Supply is not the objective — so the design aligns the bull family to the bull screens’ own standard, the strictest in the system, rather than to a diluted version of it.

A correction the document made to itself

Section 3 called the missing market-cap floor the single largest divergence. Measured on a live twenty-name catalyst list, every name was already above ₹12,835 Cr, median around ₹30,000 Cr. The floor is not binding. It is a real gap in the code and a non-event in the output, because the technical gates already select large caps.

So a size floor on the catalyst book is insurance, not a filter — worth having so a regime that starts surfacing micro-caps cannot slip through, worth nothing today. Still unmeasured for the pullback book, which reaches lower and is where a floor could actually bind.

Deliberately not proposed

  • Solvency legs on the bull family. That is the survival score’s job and the exact mismatch this design removes — a high-growth leader mid-capex can fail interest coverage. Re-adding them under another name recreates the problem.
  • Tightening the bull screens. They are already the standard everything else is being raised to.

The recovery band collisionOpen — the two halves measure “beaten down” against different highs. The signal test wants 15–35% off a 60-bar high; the regime test wants 10–40% off the 52-week high. A name 20% below its two-month high can sit 45% below its yearly one — passing the first, failing the second, and neither number means what the other reports

The remaining work is a single parser extension that unlocks four parameters at once — worth doing as one piece rather than four.

The sequencing rule, restated because it still applies

When those four legs arrive, keep the growth score as a five-check growth score and add each new leg as its own hard gate. Fold them into the score and “four of five” silently comes to mean something else, and no individual leg’s contribution stays readable in the log. The two gates already shipped follow this pattern.

12The caveat, and what it does not excuse

One partition has been run, on the growth gate, and it is reported above with its interval straddling zero. No forward-return test partitions on ownership, pledge, size or leverage. So this design is coherent — each book asks the question its premise entitles it to ask, and the bull family is held to the bull standard — but coherence is not proven edge.

Why the asymmetry decides it anyway

Given the ruling that fewer and reliable beats many and unreliable, the asymmetry matters more than the uncertainty. A stricter gate that turns out to add no alpha costs some missed trades. A loose gate that admits weak businesses into a positional book costs capital in names that would never have been picked by hand. Those are not symmetric, and the ruling says which side to err on.

The test that would settle it

Score every historical pick on both engines at its own anchor date and partition matched-horizon alpha by each. If growth separates and survival does not, the core swap is confirmed rather than argued. Half of that has now been done — the growth side. The head-to-head has not.
